World's Largest Solar Telescope Unveils Sun's Intricate 'Hidden Texture'

The Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope (DKIST) has delivered the most detailed images of the Sun's surface ever recorded, revealing its 'hidden texture' in unprecedented clarity. These observations, showcasing superheated plasma convection cells known as solar granulation and their embedded magnetic fields, are transforming our understanding of solar physics and enhancing space weather prediction capabilities. Located on Haleakalā, Maui, DKIST's 4-meter primary mirror provides a resolution 2.5 times greater than previous instruments, marking a significant advancement in astrophysics.
