US Pilot Praises F-35 to Trump During Thanksgiving Call

During a Thanksgiving Day teleconference in 2019, a US Air Force pilot flying the F-35 Lightning II told President Donald Trump that his aircraft was the 'world's greatest fighter jet.' The exchange offered a moment of operational endorsement for the F-35 program, which had previously faced scrutiny from Trump over its cost and development. The incident highlighted ongoing discussions about military procurement and the capabilities of advanced defense platforms.

### Introduction On Thanksgiving Day, November 28, 2019, an otherwise routine presidential holiday call with deployed service members garnered attention for a specific interaction. Speaking from his Mar-a-Lago resort in Florida, then-President Donald Trump connected with various U.S. military personnel serving overseas, extending holiday greetings and appreciation for their service. Among those on the call was U.S. Air Force Captain Jon 'Sniff' Van Buren, an F-35 Lightning II pilot, deployed to an undisclosed location in Southwest Asia. When asked by President Trump about the aircraft he flew, Captain Van Buren promptly identified the F-35 and declared it the 'world's greatest fighter jet.' This direct, on-the-record endorsement from an active-duty pilot during a presidential conversation offered a unique data point in the ongoing public discourse surrounding the F-35 program, an initiative that had previously been a target of presidential criticism regarding its extensive costs. ### Background The Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II is a family of single-seat, single-engine, all-weather stealth multirole combat aircraft. Designed for ground attack and air superiority missions, it comes in three main variants: the F-35A for conventional take-off and landing, the F-35B for short take-off/vertical landing, and the F-35C for carrier operations. Developed as part of the Joint Strike Fighter (JSF) program, the F-35 represents the most expensive defense acquisition program in history. Its development has been characterized by technological ambition, integrating advanced stealth capabilities, sensor fusion, and networked warfare features into a single platform. However, the program also faced significant scrutiny over its substantial cost overruns, extended development timelines, and various technical challenges. Prior to the Thanksgiving call, President Trump had publicly expressed reservations about the F-35's expense. In late 2016 and early 2017, he famously criticized the program's 'out of control' costs, leading to discussions with Lockheed Martin executives and subsequent efforts to reduce per-unit prices. These criticisms sometimes came alongside comparisons, often informal, to other advanced fighters like the F-22 Raptor, which Trump occasionally suggested was superior. ### Timeline **Early 2000s:** The F-35 Joint Strike Fighter program officially commences its development phase, aiming to create a versatile, stealth-capable fighter to replace a range of aging aircraft across U.S. and allied forces. **2006:** The first F-35 test flight occurs, initiating a long and complex flight test program. **Late 2000s - Mid 2010s:** The F-35 program experiences significant challenges, including substantial cost increases, schedule delays, and numerous technical issues, leading to widespread public and political concern. **December 2016:** Then-President-elect Donald Trump issues public statements criticizing the 'tremendous cost and cost overruns' of the F-35 program via social media. **January 2017:** Following his public statements, President Trump meets with Lockheed Martin executives at Trump Tower to discuss the F-35 program's costs, emphasizing the need for reductions. **November 28, 2019:** On Thanksgiving Day, President Trump engages in teleconferences with U.S. service members deployed abroad. **November 28, 2019:** During a call with U.S. Air Force Captain Jon 'Sniff' Van Buren, deployed to Southwest Asia, the pilot states that the F-35 Lightning II, which he flies, is the 'world's greatest fighter jet.' President Trump acknowledges the comment. ### Latest Developments Following Captain Van Buren's declaration, the immediate impact was primarily confined to media coverage of the exchange. News outlets reported on the pilot's enthusiastic endorsement, often contrasting it with the President's prior critiques of the F-35's cost efficiency. The incident served as a brief but notable highlight in the broader narrative surrounding the aircraft's operational readiness and value. No significant policy shifts or new contractual developments directly emerged as a result of this specific Thanksgiving conversation. The F-35 program continued its trajectory of production, deployment, and ongoing upgrades, driven by long-term defense strategies rather than singular anecdotal endorsements. ### Analysis Captain Van Buren's unprompted commendation of the F-35 during a presidential call offered a glimpse into the morale and professional assessment of an operational pilot. For proponents of the F-35, the statement served as validation from someone directly experiencing its capabilities in a combat-ready environment. It underscored the perception among some users that, despite its developmental hurdles, the F-35 delivers on its promise of advanced performance. President Trump's reaction, acknowledging the F-35's stealth and speed, was measured. While he had previously expressed skepticism about the jet's value for money, his response in this context allowed for an embrace of military prowess without retracting past fiscal concerns. The exchange also subtly highlighted the distinction between policy-level procurement decisions and the on-the-ground appreciation of equipment by those who operate it. The F-35's status as a 'fifth-generation' fighter rests on its stealth, sensor fusion, integrated avionics, and ability to operate within a highly networked battlespace. These attributes are often cited by military strategists and pilots as critical for modern and future conflicts, particularly against sophisticated adversaries. The public endorsement from a pilot provided a human-interest angle to these technical discussions. ### Public Response The public response to the Thanksgiving exchange was largely channeled through media reports and online commentary. Defense-focused news outlets highlighted the pilot's statement, interpreting it as a significant vote of confidence in the F-35 from an operational perspective. Social media users and defense analysts engaged in discussions weighing the pilot's viewpoint against the program's history of cost overruns and performance debates. For some, the pilot's statement reinforced a sense of national pride in advanced military technology and the capabilities of U.S. armed forces. For others, it reignited familiar debates about military spending priorities and the cost-effectiveness of high-tech defense projects. The context of a holiday call with deployed troops generally fostered a positive reception for the service members' dedication. ### Industry Impact While a single pilot's statement during a holiday call is unlikely to have direct, immediate financial or contractual repercussions for Lockheed Martin, the F-35's primary manufacturer, it contributes to the narrative surrounding the aircraft. Positive testimonials from active-duty personnel can bolster public confidence and influence perceptions among allied nations considering or operating the F-35. Such endorsements, even informal ones, can serve as valuable qualitative data points for marketing and public relations efforts within the defense industry. They provide anecdotal evidence of operational success and user satisfaction, which can be leveraged to counter criticisms focused on cost or developmental issues. The incident underscored the ongoing importance of operator feedback in shaping the long-term perception and success of major defense platforms. ### Future Outlook The F-35 program continues to evolve, with ongoing software upgrades, structural improvements, and expanded operational deployments across various U.S. military branches and international partners. The occasional public endorsements from pilots like Captain Van Buren serve to reinforce the aircraft's perceived operational value within the defense community. Future discussions around defense procurement are expected to continue balancing the strategic imperative for advanced capabilities with fiscal realities. The F-35, as a centerpiece of modern air power, will remain a focal point in these discussions. The Thanksgiving exchange provided a human element to this complex subject, connecting high-level policy with the experiences of those on the front lines. The dialogue underscored the enduring challenge of defining 'greatest' in the context of military hardware, where factors like cost, capability, and mission suitability all play a role. ### Conclusion The Thanksgiving 2019 phone call between President Donald Trump and Captain Jon Van Buren, an F-35 pilot, offered a brief but notable moment in the ongoing narrative of U.S. defense capabilities. The pilot's unequivocal declaration of the F-35 as the 'world's greatest fighter jet' provided a direct operational perspective amidst years of debate over the aircraft's cost and performance. This interaction served as a reminder of the complex interplay between political leadership, military personnel morale, and the public perception of advanced defense technology. While not altering policy, it underscored the pride and confidence that service members often hold for the equipment they operate, contributing a distinct, on-the-ground voice to the broader discussion of military readiness and procurement.
