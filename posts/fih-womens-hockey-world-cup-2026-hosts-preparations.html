Belgium and Netherlands to Co-Host FIH Women's Hockey World Cup 2026

The International Hockey Federation (FIH) has designated Belgium and the Netherlands as the joint hosts for the FIH Women's Hockey World Cup 2026. This significant announcement sets the stage for a major global sporting event in two nations renowned for their hockey prowess and organizational capabilities. The tournament, scheduled for August 2026, marks the second consecutive co-hosted women's World Cup for the Netherlands, building on the success of the 2022 edition.

## Belgium and Netherlands to Co-Host FIH Women's Hockey World Cup 2026 **LAUSANNE, SWITZERLAND** — The International Hockey Federation (FIH) has officially awarded the hosting rights for the FIH Women's Hockey World Cup 2026 to a joint bid from Belgium and the Netherlands. This decision, announced by the FIH Executive Board, positions two of the sport's most prominent nations to stage its flagship women's tournament. The tournament is anticipated to take place in August 2026, bringing together the world's elite women's national hockey teams. The joint hosting arrangement leverages the extensive experience and established sporting infrastructure of both countries, promising a high-calibre event for athletes and spectators alike. ### Background The FIH Women's Hockey World Cup stands as the pinnacle of international women's field hockey, held quadrennially and showcasing the sport's global reach and competitive spirit. Instituted in 1974, the tournament has evolved significantly, mirroring the growth and professionalisation of women's sports worldwide. Over its history, the World Cup has been instrumental in elevating the profile of women's hockey, providing a platform for national teams to vie for one of the sport's most coveted titles. The Netherlands, a dominant force in women's hockey, has a rich history with the event, having hosted it multiple times, most recently in 2022 alongside Spain. Belgium, while also a strong hockey nation with a rapidly developing women's programme, has not hosted the Women's World Cup previously. This co-hosting opportunity represents a significant moment for Belgian hockey, allowing it to share in the operational and sporting spotlight with its experienced neighbor. The 2022 edition, co-hosted by Terrassa, Spain, and Amstelveen, Netherlands, demonstrated the viability and success of a multi-nation hosting model. This precedent likely influenced the FIH's decision to again opt for a collaborative approach for 2026, aiming to maximise audience engagement and operational efficiency. ### Timeline The process for selecting the 2026 World Cup hosts began in 2022 when the FIH invited bids for both the men's and women's tournaments. National hockey federations were encouraged to submit comprehensive proposals detailing their vision, infrastructure, logistical capabilities, and financial commitments. Following a thorough evaluation period by the FIH, which included assessing bids against stringent criteria, the Executive Board convened to make its final decisions. The announcement of Belgium and the Netherlands as co-hosts for the Women's World Cup was made in November 2022. This early announcement provides the organizing committees in both nations with ample time—approximately three and a half years from the announcement date—to plan and execute the complex logistics associated with an event of this magnitude. Key milestones leading up to 2026 will include venue finalisation, detailed schedule development, and the establishment of qualification pathways. ### Latest Developments As of mid-2024, the preparatory phase for the FIH Women's Hockey World Cup 2026 is underway. While the host nations are confirmed, the specific match schedule, exact dates within August 2026, and comprehensive venue allocations are still under development by the FIH and the respective national organizing committees. Detailed information regarding ticket sales, official tournament slogans, and volunteer recruitment drives is anticipated to be released progressively throughout late 2024 and 2025. These announcements will mark key operational milestones as the event draws closer. It is important to note that the schedule, scores, results, and points table for the tournament itself will only become available once the event commences in August 2026. The qualification process, which will determine the participating nations, will also unfold over the coming two years, typically involving continental championships and dedicated World Cup qualifier tournaments. ### Analysis The selection of Belgium and the Netherlands as co-hosts is a strategic move by the FIH. Both nations possess state-of-the-art hockey facilities, a passionate fan base, and deep-rooted hockey cultures. The Netherlands, in particular, has a proven track record of successfully staging major international hockey events. This partnership not only ensures a high standard of tournament delivery but also reinforces the sport's strong presence in Western Europe. The geographical proximity of the two nations facilitates logistical planning and offers convenience for fans who may wish to attend matches in both countries. The decision also reflects a broader trend in international sports to leverage shared resources and expertise through co-hosting, mitigating financial risks and maximizing public engagement. For Belgium, it presents an opportunity to elevate its women's hockey program and inspire a new generation of athletes. ### Public Response The initial public response to the announcement has been largely positive within the global hockey community, particularly in Belgium and the Netherlands. Federations, athletes, and fans have expressed enthusiasm for the upcoming event, anticipating a vibrant and competitive tournament. Media outlets in both host countries covered the announcement extensively, highlighting the prestige of hosting a World Cup. There is a general sense of anticipation for detailed planning announcements, especially regarding specific cities and venues where matches will be played. Public interest is expected to build significantly as more concrete details emerge, such as match schedules, participating teams, and ticketing information. Social media channels and national hockey forums indicate a strong desire among fans to engage with the event as planning progresses. ### Industry Impact Hosting an event of the scale of the FIH Women's Hockey World Cup has substantial economic and industry implications for the host nations. The influx of teams, support staff, media, and international spectators is expected to generate significant revenue through tourism, hospitality, and local services. Sponsorship opportunities are likely to attract both national and international brands, eager to associate with a prominent women's sporting event. Media rights for broadcasting the tournament will also represent a considerable value, ensuring global visibility for the sport and the host countries. Beyond direct economic benefits, the event can serve as a catalyst for investment in sports infrastructure, potentially leading to upgrades in training facilities and stadiums. This long-term legacy benefits local communities and future generations of athletes. ### Future Outlook Over the next two years, the focus for the FIH and the host organizing committees will be on finalizing all operational aspects. This includes confirming specific venues, developing the full match schedule, outlining detailed security protocols, and launching comprehensive marketing and ticketing campaigns. On the sporting front, the qualification pathways for the 16 participating nations will be a significant development. Teams will battle through continental championships and FIH Hockey World Cup Qualifiers to secure their spots, building excitement and competitive narratives leading up to August 2026. The legacy of the 2026 World Cup is also a key consideration. Both Belgium and the Netherlands aim to use the event to further popularize field hockey, particularly among women and youth, and to inspire greater participation and investment in the sport. ### Conclusion With Belgium and the Netherlands at the helm, the FIH Women's Hockey World Cup 2026 is poised to be a landmark event. The partnership of two strong hockey nations, coupled with meticulous planning over the coming years, promises a world-class tournament that will celebrate the athleticism and growing appeal of women's field hockey. The journey to August 2026 will be marked by anticipation and unfolding details, culminating in a showcase of global sporting excellence.
