One Year Countdown to Major IUCN Congress Emphasizes Urgency for Protected Areas

With approximately one year remaining until the next major global gathering of the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN), the focus intensifies on the critical role of protected and conserved areas in addressing biodiversity loss and climate change. This period marks a pivotal moment for governments, conservationists, indigenous peoples, and stakeholders worldwide to accelerate progress towards ambitious global targets, particularly the '30x30' goal of protecting 30% of the planet by 2030, and to refine strategies for effective and equitable conservation. The upcoming congress serves as a crucial platform for assessing current challenges, sharing best practices, and forging the necessary partnerships to safeguard the natural world.

### Introduction The global conservation community is now on a roughly one-year countdown to the next major congress convened by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN). This significant event, anticipated to be the World Conservation Congress, represents a crucial juncture for evaluating the progress and charting the future course for protected and conserved areas worldwide. The impending gathering underscores the escalating urgency to address biodiversity loss and the impacts of climate change. Preparations are intensifying as nations and organizations refine their strategies to meet ambitious global targets. Central to these discussions is the '30x30' goal, enshrined in the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, which aims to protect 30% of the planet's land and sea by 2030. The congress will serve as a vital forum for assessing current conservation efforts and identifying pathways to accelerate action. ### Background The IUCN, established in 1948, is the world's largest and most diverse environmental network, bringing together over 1,400 member organizations, including states, government agencies, and NGOs. It provides a neutral platform for governments, scientists, and civil society to share information and work together for conservation. Major IUCN congresses, particularly the World Conservation Congress (WCC), are held approximately every four years. These events are crucial for setting global conservation agendas, developing policies, and influencing international environmental governance. The last WCC, held in Marseille, France, in September 2021, produced significant resolutions impacting global biodiversity efforts. While the WCC encompasses all aspects of conservation, the management and expansion of protected and conserved areas consistently form a core pillar of its agenda. Historically, dedicated events like the IUCN World Parks Congress (last held in Sydney in 2014) have focused exclusively on protected area issues, highlighting their specialized importance within the broader conservation movement. ### Timeline **2014:** The last dedicated IUCN World Parks Congress was held in Sydney, Australia. This event produced the 'Promise of Sydney', a vision for the future of protected areas. **2016:** The IUCN World Conservation Congress convened in Hawaii, USA. Discussions focused on nature-based solutions and connectivity for conservation. **September 2021:** The delayed IUCN World Conservation Congress took place in Marseille, France. Key outcomes included commitments to post-2020 global biodiversity targets. **December 2022:** The Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (GBF) was adopted by 196 countries under the Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD). Target 3 of the GBF established the ambitious '30x30' goal. **Late 2023/Early 2024:** The approximate one-year mark begins for intensive preparations leading up to the next major IUCN Congress, expected around 2025. **2030:** The deadline for achieving the 30x30 global biodiversity target, a central benchmark for future conservation success. ### Latest Developments The adoption of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework has invigorated global efforts to expand and enhance protected and conserved areas. The '30x30' target has become a guiding principle for national conservation strategies, driving new initiatives to designate and manage ecologically significant sites. Many countries are currently undergoing assessments of their existing protected area networks and identifying gaps in coverage, particularly for marine and inland water ecosystems. The role of Other Effective Area-Based Conservation Measures (OECMs), which recognize conservation efforts outside formally designated protected areas, is gaining prominence as a complementary approach. However, progress is uneven. While the total extent of protected areas has grown, concerns persist regarding the effectiveness of management, the equitable involvement of indigenous peoples and local communities, and securing adequate, sustainable financing for these vital spaces. ### Analysis The upcoming IUCN Congress faces the dual challenge of accelerating the expansion of protected and conserved areas while simultaneously improving their quality and effectiveness. Mere designation is insufficient; areas must be effectively managed, well-connected, and equitably governed to deliver biodiversity outcomes and essential ecosystem services. Discussions are expected to delve into innovative financing mechanisms, including nature-based solutions and private sector engagement. The role of indigenous peoples and local communities (IPLCs) in conservation is paramount, with a strong emphasis on rights-based approaches and co-management models that leverage traditional ecological knowledge. The nexus between protected areas and climate change will also be a central analytical theme. Protected areas can serve as crucial natural buffers against climate impacts and as carbon sinks, highlighting their importance for both adaptation and mitigation strategies. Integrating climate resilience into protected area planning will be critical. ### Public Response Public awareness and support for protected areas generally remain high across many regions, driven by increasing concerns about environmental degradation and the impacts of climate change. Non-governmental organizations (NGOs) continue to play a significant role in advocating for stronger protections, raising public awareness, and mobilizing citizen science initiatives. Local communities, particularly those living near or within protected areas, often demonstrate a strong commitment to conservation when their rights, livelihoods, and cultural values are respected and integrated into management plans. However, instances of conflict can arise if top-down approaches ignore local needs or traditional land uses, underscoring the importance of inclusive governance. Social media campaigns and educational programs are also contributing to a broader understanding of the benefits of protected areas, from preserving wildlife to providing clean air and water, and supporting recreational opportunities. ### Industry Impact The expansion and improved management of protected areas have diverse impacts on various industries. The sustainable tourism sector, for instance, heavily relies on healthy, well-managed natural spaces, creating economic opportunities and employment for local communities adjacent to these areas. For industries reliant on natural resources, such as forestry and fisheries, protected areas can act as critical reservoirs for biodiversity, supporting sustainable practices in surrounding buffer zones. Businesses are increasingly recognizing the 'nature positive' agenda, aligning their operations with conservation goals through corporate social responsibility initiatives and investments in ecological restoration. Conversely, some extractive industries or large-scale infrastructure projects may face restrictions or increased scrutiny within or near protected areas, leading to calls for responsible planning and environmental impact assessments that prioritize conservation values. The congress will likely foster dialogue on balancing economic development with ecological protection. ### Future Outlook The upcoming IUCN Congress is poised to significantly shape the implementation phase of the 30x30 target. Experts anticipate a strong focus on practical implementation strategies, capacity building, and mechanisms for accountability. The effective integration of marine protected areas will be a key area of discussion, given the relatively lower protection levels for oceanic ecosystems. Looking ahead, success will hinge on robust political will, innovative financing solutions, and genuine partnerships across all sectors. The congress offers a critical opportunity to refine metrics for 'effective management' and 'equitable governance,' ensuring that protected areas not only expand in size but also deliver tangible, lasting conservation outcomes for nature and people. While the exact dates and host for the next World Conservation Congress are part of ongoing planning, the 'one year countdown' period serves as a crucial window for stakeholders to consolidate their positions, develop concrete proposals, and build consensus on the path forward. The decisions made will profoundly influence global biodiversity targets for decades to come. ### Conclusion The approaching major IUCN Congress marks a critical period for global conservation. With the ambition of the 30x30 target driving much of the discourse, the gathering will be instrumental in translating global commitments into tangible action on the ground. The next year will be defined by intensive preparations, strategic dialogue, and collaborative efforts to ensure that protected and conserved areas effectively safeguard the planet's irreplaceable biodiversity. This global convergence offers a vital platform to address complex challenges, from financing and governance to climate resilience and indigenous rights. The outcomes of this congress will undoubtedly steer international conservation policy, determining the trajectory for achieving a nature-positive future and securing essential ecosystem services for all.
