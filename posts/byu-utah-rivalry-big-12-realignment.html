BYU-Utah Rivalry Reenters Conference Play Amid Big 12 Realignment

The historic 'Holy War' rivalry between Brigham Young University and the University of Utah is set for a dramatic new chapter. After more than a decade of competing in separate conferences, the two institutions will once again face off as direct conference rivals within the Big 12, beginning in 2024. This reunion, driven by extensive conference realignment, fundamentally shifts the dynamics of one of college football's most passionate contests, adding conference championship implications and renewing institutional competition on a broader scale.

The 'Holy War,' the fiercely contested rivalry between Brigham Young University (BYU) and the University of Utah, is poised to enter a significant new era. For the first time since 2010, these long-standing adversaries will meet on the athletic fields as direct conference competitors, a development spurred by the dramatic and ongoing restructuring of NCAA Division I athletics. This reunion within the Big 12 Conference marks a pivotal moment for both institutions and their respective fan bases. The rivalry, which has endured for well over a century, will now carry the added weight of conference standings, championship aspirations, and the heightened prestige of intra-conference competition. ### Background The rivalry between BYU, located in Provo, and the University of Utah, situated in Salt Lake City, is deeply ingrained in the cultural fabric of the state. Separated by approximately 45 miles, the two universities have cultivated a competitive spirit that transcends sport, touching upon academic, social, and even religious facets of life in Utah. The initial football contest between the two institutions dates back to 1896. Over the decades, they competed across various leagues, including the Rocky Mountain Athletic Conference, the Western Athletic Conference (WAC), and were founding members of the Mountain West Conference (MWC) in 1999. This shared conference history provided regular, high-stakes matchups. ### Timeline The landscape of the rivalry underwent a profound change in 2011. The University of Utah accepted an invitation to join the then-powerful Pac-12 Conference, elevating its athletic profile on a national stage. Simultaneously, BYU made the strategic decision to become an FBS independent in football, allowing greater flexibility in scheduling and national exposure. Its other sports programs joined the West Coast Conference (WCC). This divergence meant the end of automatic annual football matchups within a conference structure. Despite the conference split, the rivalry's intensity ensured its continuation. Both universities committed to scheduling non-conference football games, maintaining the tradition. These matchups, while competitive, lacked the conference championship implications that had historically defined their contests. During this period of separation, Utah developed a notable winning streak in football, underscoring its success in the Pac-12. BYU, as an independent, navigated a challenging schedule, facing a diverse array of opponents from across the country. ### Latest Developments The most recent wave of conference realignment has reshaped the national collegiate athletic landscape. In 2021, BYU accepted an invitation to join the Big 12 Conference, officially commencing its membership on July 1, 2023. This move provided BYU with a stable conference home and access to Power Five resources and competition. The Pac-12 Conference, which Utah had called home for over a decade, experienced a significant exodus of its member institutions. In August 2023, Utah, alongside Arizona, Arizona State, and Colorado, announced its decision to depart the Pac-12 and join the Big 12 Conference. This move becomes effective on August 2, 2024. This collective realignment brings BYU and Utah back together under the same conference banner. The 2024-2025 athletic year will feature both institutions competing as Big 12 members, ensuring their historic rivalry will once again contribute directly to conference standings and championship races. ### Analysis The re-establishment of the BYU-Utah rivalry as a conference fixture is more than a simple scheduling change; it is a fundamental recalibration of institutional priorities and competitive stakes. For fans, it reignites the direct competition that makes rivalries so compelling, where every game holds dual significance—for bragging rights and for the path to a conference title. From an athletic department perspective, the Big 12 offers a new operational framework. Both universities will now share broadcast revenue streams, benefit from collective marketing, and face a consistent set of opponents across all sports. This stability contrasts sharply with the uncertainty that characterized the final years of the Pac-12. The move also provides both programs with a clear and stable pathway to the College Football Playoff under its expanded format, as the Big 12 remains a prominent Power Four conference. The rivalry games will now directly influence their standing within this competitive structure. ### Public Response The announcement of Utah joining BYU in the Big 12 was met with widespread enthusiasm from both fan bases. While the intense competition and often contentious nature of the 'Holy War' are well-known, there is an underlying appreciation for the direct confrontation that only a shared conference can provide. Social media platforms buzzed with reactions, and local sports talk radio dedicated extensive coverage to the implications. Many expressed relief that the rivalry would resume its full vigor, free from the complexities of non-conference scheduling and with the added weight of championship contention. The prospect of annual matchups across multiple sports, beyond just football, also fueled excitement. ### Industry Impact The convergence of BYU and Utah in the Big 12 represents a significant development for the broader college sports industry. It further solidifies the Big 12's footprint in the Western United States and adds two strong brands with passionate fan bases to its roster. Media rights holders will likely view this reunion as a valuable asset. The guaranteed annual matchups between two intense rivals are compelling content, driving viewership and engagement. This could potentially enhance the Big 12's overall media valuation in future negotiations. The local economies in both Provo and Salt Lake City also stand to benefit from increased fan travel and game-day expenditures associated with renewed conference rivalry games. ### Future Outlook The future of the BYU-Utah rivalry within the Big 12 promises a return to its traditional high-stakes form. The guaranteed annual contest in football, alongside regular competition in basketball and other sports, will ensure the 'Holy War' remains a focal point of the athletic calendar. Recruiting battles in Utah and surrounding states are likely to intensify, with both programs now pitching the same conference affiliation to prospective athletes. The ability to guarantee a game against a direct rival within the same conference could be a significant draw for recruits. While the immediate focus is on the athletic competition, the move also presents opportunities for non-athletic collaboration between the two universities, such as academic partnerships or research initiatives, though competition remains the primary driver. ### Conclusion The BYU-Utah rivalry, born from proximity and a deep competitive spirit, has navigated more than a decade of separate conference affiliations. The 'brave new world' of college athletics, marked by unprecedented conference realignment, has unexpectedly brought these two institutions back into direct conference competition. This reunion in the Big 12 Conference not only revitalizes one of college sports' most storied rivalries but also fundamentally alters its nature. The return of conference championship implications ensures that every future 'Holy War' will be fought with renewed fervor and heightened significance, continuing a tradition that defines collegiate sports in Utah.
