FIDE's Inaugural Youth Leadership Forum Cultivates Global Minds

The International Chess Federation (FIDE) recently convened its inaugural World Youth Leadership Forum (WYLF), an initiative designed to harness the strategic thinking inherent in chess to foster global youth leadership. Held from October 23-27, 2023, in a hybrid format centered in Budapest, Hungary, the forum brought together 100 young leaders from over 80 countries. Participants engaged in intensive workshops and discussions focused on ethical leadership, public advocacy, and project management, signaling a strategic shift for FIDE to extend its influence beyond competitive play into broader social and educational development.

### FIDE's Inaugural Youth Leadership Forum Cultivates Global Minds **Budapest, Hungary** – The International Chess Federation (FIDE) has inaugurated its ambitious World Youth Leadership Forum (WYLF), a pioneering initiative aimed at nurturing the next generation of global leaders. Held from October 23 to 27, 2023, the forum brought together 100 young minds from over 80 countries in a hybrid format, with a central hub in Budapest, Hungary, and significant online participation. The WYLF marks a significant expansion of FIDE's role, moving beyond its traditional focus on competitive chess to leverage the game's intrinsic values for broader societal development. The forum’s core objective was to equip young individuals with critical leadership skills, foster cross-cultural understanding, and empower them to drive positive change in their communities and on the global stage. FIDE President Arkady Dvorkovich, in his opening remarks, underscored the federation's commitment to youth development. He highlighted how chess, a game demanding strategic foresight, adaptability, and ethical decision-making, provides an ideal foundation for leadership training. The forum aimed to translate these skills from the chessboard into real-world application. The genesis of the World Youth Leadership Forum lies in FIDE's evolving strategic vision. Recognizing the global reach and intellectual demands of chess, the federation sought to broaden its impact beyond merely organizing tournaments. The idea emerged from a desire to utilize chess as a platform for educational and social development, aligning with global agendas for youth empowerment and sustainable development. Over recent years, FIDE has progressively increased its engagement in social responsibility initiatives. Programs focused on chess in education, promoting gender equality in the sport, and advocating for chess as a tool for cognitive development in various age groups laid the groundwork. The WYLF represents a culmination of these efforts, formalizing a dedicated platform for leadership cultivation among young people. FIDE leadership noted that the forum was designed to tap into the analytical and problem-solving abilities that many young chess players naturally develop, channeling these talents towards practical leadership challenges. The ambition was to create a network of future leaders who could apply their strategic acumen to complex global issues. The concept for the WYLF was first announced in early 2023 as a key component of FIDE’s strategic plan for youth development. Following the initial announcement, a global call for applications was launched in March 2023, inviting young individuals aged 18-25 with demonstrated leadership potential and an interest in global affairs to apply. A rigorous selection process, evaluating candidates based on their academic achievements, extracurricular involvement, leadership experiences, and essays on global challenges, concluded in July 2023. This led to the final selection of 100 participants from a diverse array of countries and backgrounds. The inaugural forum officially commenced on October 23, 2023. The five-day program, which ran until October 27, featured an intensive schedule of workshops, keynote addresses, and interactive sessions. Prominent experts from international organizations, academia, and the private sector led discussions on topics ranging from ethical leadership to cultural diplomacy. The 'Latest Developments' section highlights the immediate outcomes and activities of the forum. Participants engaged in a series of thematic workshops covering areas such as strategic planning, public advocacy, project management, and the principles of sustainable development. These sessions were designed to be highly interactive, encouraging peer-to-peer learning and collaborative problem-solving. One significant aspect was the 'Global Challenges Project,' where participants were grouped to develop innovative solutions to pressing contemporary issues. These projects were presented on the final day, showcasing the collaborative spirit and practical application of the skills learned. FIDE President Dvorkovich, alongside other senior FIDE officials, attended the closing ceremony, commending the participants on their dedication and visionary ideas. The World Youth Leadership Forum represents a strategic pivot for FIDE. By investing in youth leadership, FIDE aims to broaden its relevance beyond the competitive chess circuit, positioning itself as a key player in global youth development. This initiative could potentially enhance the perception of chess as not just a sport or a game, but a potent educational tool and a pathway to leadership. The forum's hybrid model also demonstrated FIDE's adaptability to modern global communication, ensuring accessibility for a wide range of international participants. This approach allowed for diverse perspectives and experiences to enrich the discussions, fostering a truly global dialogue on leadership. Initial public response to the WYLF has been largely positive. Participants universally expressed appreciation for the unique opportunity to connect with peers from diverse backgrounds and learn from accomplished mentors. Social media channels and internal feedback mechanisms indicated high levels of engagement and satisfaction with the program's content and delivery. Educational institutions and youth organizations have also lauded FIDE's initiative, viewing it as a valuable addition to the global landscape of youth development programs. Several participants highlighted the practical applicability of the skills acquired, particularly in critical thinking and collaborative problem-solving, which they attributed to the forum's chess-inspired methodology. The success of FIDE's inaugural WYLF could serve as a significant precedent for other international sports federations and cultural organizations. It demonstrates a model where the inherent values and disciplines of a particular field—in this case, chess—can be leveraged to cultivate broader life skills and leadership qualities. This could inspire similar initiatives across various sectors. Such programs have the potential to redefine the role of international federations, moving them beyond purely regulatory or competitive functions towards becoming integral contributors to global human capital development. This paradigm shift could lead to increased collaboration between sports bodies, educational institutions, and governmental agencies on youth-focused initiatives. FIDE has expressed a clear commitment to making the World Youth Leadership Forum an annual event. Plans are already underway to incorporate feedback from the inaugural cohort to refine and expand future iterations. The federation aims to grow the network of WYLF alumni, fostering a sustained global community of young leaders. The long-term vision includes developing regional leadership hubs, offering specialized tracks within the forum, and establishing a robust mentorship program that connects alumni with seasoned leaders. FIDE leadership anticipates that the forum will continue to produce impactful projects and foster leaders who can effectively address the multifaceted challenges of the 21st century. The inaugural World Youth Leadership Forum stands as a testament to FIDE's vision for a future where chess transcends the board, serving as a powerful catalyst for developing well-rounded, ethically minded, and globally aware leaders. By investing in its youth, FIDE is not only securing the future of chess but also contributing meaningfully to the broader tapestry of global leadership development.
