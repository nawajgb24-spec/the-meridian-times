Wise Old Grandpa, Potentially World's Oldest Llama, Dies at 31

Wise Old Grandpa, a llama believed by his caretakers to be the oldest of his kind globally, has passed away at the remarkable age of 31 at The Llama Sanctuary in Chase, British Columbia. His death marks the end of an extraordinary life that far surpassed the typical llama lifespan, drawing attention to animal longevity and dedicated care. While his 'world's oldest' title remains unconfirmed by official record-keeping bodies, his story has resonated widely, highlighting the deep bond between animals and their human caregivers.

The Llama Sanctuary in Chase, British Columbia, has announced the peaceful passing of Wise Old Grandpa, a male llama who reached the extraordinary age of 31. His caretakers, Richard and Lori Ann Smedley, co-founders of the sanctuary, have long considered him to be potentially the world's oldest living llama, a claim that, while unverified by official record bodies, underscores his remarkable longevity. Wise Old Grandpa’s life extended well beyond the typical lifespan for llamas, which generally ranges from 15 to 25 years. His extended existence captivated animal enthusiasts and brought global attention to the dedicated care provided at The Llama Sanctuary, a non-profit organization focused on rescuing, rehabilitating, and providing a permanent home for neglected and abused llamas.
