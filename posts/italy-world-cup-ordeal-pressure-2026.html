Italy's World Cup Ordeal: A Nation's Demand for Action After Repeated Absences

Italian football grapples with the fallout of missing two consecutive FIFA World Cups, a crisis prompting urgent calls for systemic change. This article examines the complex legacy of Roberto Mancini's tenure, from Euro 2020 triumph to 2022 World Cup qualification failure and subsequent departure. With Luciano Spalletti now at the helm, the pressure to avoid an unprecedented third consecutive absence in 2026 defines a pivotal era for the four-time champions.

Italy, a nation steeped in footballing glory, finds itself at a critical crossroads, grappling with the unprecedented reality of missing two consecutive FIFA World Cups. The azure blue of its national team, once synonymous with global dominance and four world titles, now faces an existential challenge: how to reclaim its place among football's elite and, more pressingly, avoid the catastrophic prospect of a third consecutive absence from the sport's pinnacle tournament. The recent past has been a tumultuous journey marked by both euphoria and profound disappointment. Following the shock failure to qualify for the 2018 World Cup, the Italian Football Federation (FIGC) embarked on a significant overhaul, turning to Roberto Mancini, a manager renowned for his tactical acumen and winning pedigree. His appointment in 2018 was met with cautious optimism, tasked with restoring national pride and rebuilding a team that had lost its identity. Mancini’s initial tenure brought a remarkable resurgence. He guided Italy to an improbable triumph at UEFA Euro 2020 (played in 2021), captivating fans with an attractive, attacking style of play and setting a new national record of 37 matches unbeaten. The victory was hailed as a renaissance, a clear signal that the Azzurri were back on the global stage, their earlier World Cup humiliation firmly in the rearview mirror. However, the highs proved fleeting. Just months after their European glory, Italy stunningly failed to qualify for the 2022 FIFA World Cup, suffering a devastating 1-0 defeat to underdog North Macedonia in a playoff semi-final. This second consecutive World Cup absence plunged Italian football back into a state of crisis, reopening wounds that many believed had healed. **Background: A Legacy Tarnished** Italy's rich footballing heritage includes World Cup victories in 1934, 1938, 1982, and 2006. For decades, World Cup qualification was almost a given, a testament to the nation's deep talent pool and tactical prowess. The failure to qualify for the 1958 tournament was an anomaly, a distant memory that few thought would be revisited in the modern era. The 2018 miss, under coach Gian Piero Ventura, was a profound shock. It signaled deeper systemic issues within Italian football, prompting introspection and the immediate appointment of Mancini. His mission was clear: restore pride, instill a winning mentality, and integrate a new generation of talent. The Euro 2020 victory was the spectacular fulfillment of this mission, momentarily masking the underlying fragilities. The subsequent collapse in the 2022 World Cup qualifiers, particularly the inability to overcome North Macedonia and then Portugal in the final playoff round, starkly exposed these unresolved issues, leading to widespread disbelief and anger. **Timeline: The Rollercoaster Years** * **November 2017:** Italy fails to qualify for the 2018 FIFA World Cup after a playoff defeat to Sweden, ending a 60-year streak of participation. * **May 2018:** Roberto Mancini is appointed head coach, tasked with rebuilding the national team. * **July 2021:** Italy, under Mancini, wins UEFA Euro 2020, defeating England in the final. The team also sets a new world record of 37 consecutive international matches without defeat. * **March 2022:** Italy loses 1-0 to North Macedonia in the 2022 FIFA World Cup qualifying playoff semi-final, failing to qualify for a second consecutive World Cup. * **August 13, 2023:** Roberto Mancini resigns unexpectedly as Italy's head coach, citing personal reasons and reports of disagreements with the FIGC. * **August 18, 2023:** Luciano Spalletti, fresh from winning Serie A with Napoli, is appointed as Mancini's successor. * **August 27, 2023:** Roberto Mancini is confirmed as the new head coach of the Saudi Arabia national team. **Latest Developments: Spalletti's Mandate** The abrupt departure of Roberto Mancini in August 2023, just weeks before crucial Euro 2024 qualifiers, sent further tremors through Italian football. FIGC President Gabriele Gravina moved swiftly, appointing Luciano Spalletti, who had just led Napoli to their first Serie A title in 33 years. Spalletti's mandate is clear: stabilize the national team, ensure qualification for Euro 2024, and, most critically, build a squad capable of securing a berth at the 2026 FIFA World Cup. Spalletti has emphasized renewed commitment, tactical flexibility, and fostering a strong team spirit. The immediate challenge has been integrating new players while maintaining competitive results in the ongoing qualification campaigns. The pressure on Spalletti and his squad is immense, as the spectre of a third World Cup absence looms large. **Analysis: Deep-Rooted Challenges and Lessons from the Mancini Era** The repeated World Cup qualification failures point to challenges beyond individual coaching decisions. Issues such as the development pathway for young Italian players, the reliance on foreign talent in Serie A, and a perceived lack of tactical innovation at youth levels have been frequently cited. The Mancini era, while delivering the Euro 2020 high, also highlighted the fine margins in international football and the unforgiving nature of knockout qualifications. Mancini's initial appointment was a direct response to the 2018 failure, a deliberate move to bring back a respected figure to instill discipline and ambition. His tactical flexibility and ability to integrate younger players were commendable. However, the subsequent 2022 qualification failure underscored that even a European triumph cannot guarantee consistency, especially when underlying structural issues persist. The sudden resignation further complicated the narrative, leaving many questions unanswered about the internal dynamics of the national setup. **Public Response: Frustration and Fervour** For the passionate Italian populace, football is more than a sport; it is an integral part of national identity. The repeated World Cup absences have been met with a mixture of disbelief, anger, and deep sadness. Fans who celebrated wildly in 2021 now express profound disillusionment, fearing that Italy's standing in world football is rapidly diminishing. Calls for systemic reform within the FIGC and Serie A are widespread, reflecting a national yearning for sustained success and stability. While the immediate focus is on Euro 2024, the shadow of the 2026 World Cup qualification hangs heavy. The prospect of a third consecutive miss is almost unthinkable for many, fueling a fervour for action and demanding tangible results from Spalletti's squad. **Industry Impact: Economic and Cultural Reverberations** Missing consecutive World Cups has significant ramifications beyond mere sporting disappointment. Economically, the absence impacts television rights, sponsorship deals, and merchandise sales. The national team brand, a powerful marketing tool, suffers, affecting the broader Italian football ecosystem, including Serie A clubs and youth academies. Culturally, it dampens national morale and reduces youth engagement with the sport, potentially impacting the talent pipeline for future generations. Participation in major tournaments provides a crucial platform for player development and exposure, allowing young talents to gain invaluable experience. Its absence creates a void that is difficult to fill, affecting both individual careers and the national team's long-term competitiveness. **Future Outlook: Rebuilding for 2026** The road ahead for Italian football is challenging but clear: ensure qualification for the 2026 FIFA World Cup. Under Luciano Spalletti, the FIGC is focusing on a renewed emphasis on player development, tactical evolution, and fostering a cohesive national identity. The hope is that Spalletti’s proven ability to inspire teams and implement clear tactical frameworks will translate into consistent performances on the international stage. Long-term strategies include strengthening youth academies, revisiting league regulations to provide more opportunities for Italian players, and ensuring a stable coaching environment. The goal is not just to qualify for 2026 but to build a sustainable structure that prevents future recurrences of such a profound sporting crisis. **Conclusion: A Crucial Juncture** Italian football stands at a pivotal moment. The echoes of past glory are faint, overshadowed by the stark reality of recent World Cup failures. The complex legacy of Roberto Mancini, a figure who delivered historic triumph only to preside over renewed World Cup heartbreak, serves as a poignant reminder of the fine margins in elite sports. As the nation rallies behind Luciano Spalletti, the imperative to avoid a third consecutive World Cup absence is not merely a sporting ambition but a cultural necessity, demanding immediate and sustained action to restore Italy's rightful place on the global football stage.
