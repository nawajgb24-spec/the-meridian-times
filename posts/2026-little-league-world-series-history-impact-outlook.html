Stotz's vision was to provide a structured, supervised environment for children to play baseball, emphasizing fair play and community involvement. The first official Little League game was played on June 6, 1939. The initiative quickly gained traction, and by 1947, the first National Little League Tournament was held in Williamsport. Three teams competed for the inaugural title, a far cry from the international spectacle it is today. This early success laid the groundwork for what would become one of the most beloved and recognized youth sporting events globally, attracting both participants and spectators from diverse backgrounds. Over the years, the tournament expanded its reach beyond the United States. In 1957, Monterrey, Mexico, made history by becoming the first international team to win the series, signaling the event's burgeoning global appeal. This victory underscored the tournament's potential as a platform for cultural exchange and international goodwill, moving beyond a purely national competition to a worldwide phenomenon. ### Timeline **1939:** Carl Stotz organizes the first Little League season in Williamsport, Pennsylvania, with three teams. **1947:** The first National Little League Tournament, now recognized as the inaugural Little League World Series, is held in Williamsport.
