Jesuits Prioritize Global Listening Amidst Evolving Mission

The Society of Jesus has reaffirmed its foundational commitment to deep listening, both internally and externally, as it navigates complex global challenges. Guided by its Universal Apostolic Preferences and actively participating in the Church's Synod on Synodality, the Jesuit order is fostering a culture of discernment to better serve its mission and engage with a world in flux. This approach emphasizes hearing the voices of the marginalized, youth, and those advocating for environmental justice, reflecting a strategic adaptation to contemporary realities under the leadership of Superior General Fr. Arturo Sosa and the influential example of Pope Francis.

### Introduction The Society of Jesus, a global religious order within the Catholic Church, has placed a renewed and profound emphasis on the practice of listening. This is not merely a passive reception of information but an active, discerning engagement with the diverse voices and pressing concerns of the modern world. This strategic prioritization of 'listening to the world speaking' underscores a foundational aspect of Ignatian spirituality and serves as a guiding principle for the Jesuits' evolving mission in the 21st century. This commitment is particularly evident in two major initiatives: the Society's own Universal Apostolic Preferences (UAPs) and its enthusiastic participation in the Catholic Church's global Synod on Synodality. Both frameworks call for an attentive ear to the cries of the earth and the poor, the aspirations of youth, and the spiritual yearning for deeper connection with God. ### Background The Society of Jesus, founded by St. Ignatius of Loyola in 1540, has a long history of intellectual rigor, global mission, and engagement with contemporary issues. Its members, known as Jesuits, have historically been at the forefront of education, scientific inquiry, and social justice advocacy across continents. Central to Ignatian spirituality is the concept of discernment – a process of prayerful reflection and listening to God's will, often revealed through human experience and the 'signs of the times.' This tradition informs the Jesuit approach to mission, encouraging adaptability and a readiness to respond to urgent needs. In recent decades, the Society has grappled with the implications of globalization, secularization, and environmental crises. This introspection culminated in a significant re-evaluation of its global priorities, leading to the articulation of the Universal Apostolic Preferences. ### Timeline **2016:** Fr. Arturo Sosa Abascal, S.J., is elected the 31st Superior General of the Society of Jesus. His leadership immediately initiates a process of communal discernment to identify the Society's global priorities. **2017-2019:** An extensive consultation process takes place across the global Jesuit network, involving thousands of Jesuits and their collaborators. This period is characterized by deep listening to local realities, emerging challenges, and the spiritual movements within the Society itself. **2019:** Following this discernment, Fr. Arturo Sosa promulgates the four Universal Apostolic Preferences (UAPs) for the period 2019-2029. These preferences – promoting discernment and the Spiritual Exercises, walking with the excluded, journeying with youth, and caring for our common home – are presented as guiding stars for the Society's global mission. **October 2021:** Pope Francis formally inaugurates the global Synod on Synodality, with its theme "For a Synodal Church: Communion, Participation, and Mission." This worldwide process explicitly calls for an unprecedented level of listening and dialogue within the entire Catholic Church, echoing the Jesuit emphasis. **2021-Present:** Jesuit provinces, communities, and institutions worldwide actively integrate the UAPs into their ministries and participate significantly in the local, national, and continental phases of the Synod on Synodality, contributing insights drawn from their long tradition of discerning engagement with the world. ### Latest Developments The Jesuit commitment to listening remains a dynamic and evolving aspect of its operations. Recent reports from Jesuit provinces highlight various initiatives, including participatory workshops with young people, dialogues with indigenous communities, and collaborative projects addressing ecological concerns. Fr. Arturo Sosa has consistently emphasized that the UAPs are not merely programs but a way of proceeding – a call to constant discernment rooted in a posture of listening. This approach has led to a re-evaluation of existing projects and the initiation of new ones that directly respond to the voices heard. Furthermore, the Society's engagement with the Synod on Synodality has involved providing theological and spiritual accompaniment, organizing listening sessions, and contributing reflections that draw on Ignatian principles of spiritual conversation. This engagement is seen as a natural extension of their internal processes of discernment. ### Analysis The Jesuits' intensified focus on listening is multifaceted. Theologically, it aligns with their charism of 'finding God in all things,' which necessitates an open and reflective posture towards human experience. Practically, it represents a strategic adaptation to a rapidly changing world, recognizing that effective mission requires a deep understanding of contemporary challenges and diverse perspectives. This approach also reflects the influence of Pope Francis, himself a Jesuit, whose pontificate has consistently emphasized encounter, dialogue, and a 'listening Church.' His call for synodality resonates deeply with the Ignatian practice of communal discernment and the UAPs' call to walk alongside humanity in its struggles and hopes. The UAPs, born from an internal listening process, provide a clear framework for action, but their implementation requires continuous external listening. This ensures that the Society's efforts remain relevant and responsive, avoiding insularity and embracing the complexity of global realities. ### Public Response The Jesuit emphasis on listening has been largely well-received within the broader Catholic Church and by secular observers. Within Catholic circles, it is often seen as a significant contribution to the Church's ongoing efforts to modernize and become more responsive to its members and the wider world. Many commend the Jesuits for modeling a process of institutional self-reflection and adaptation. The explicit alignment with Pope Francis's synodal vision has also garnered positive attention, reinforcing the sense of a unified direction within key segments of the Church. However, the challenge remains in translating this ethos of listening into tangible structural changes and ensuring that all voices, particularly those historically marginalized, are genuinely heard and integrated into decision-making processes, rather than simply acknowledged. ### Industry Impact Interpreting 'Industry Impact' within the context of a religious order, the Jesuits' listening approach has had a notable influence on the broader religious landscape and the non-profit sector. Their methodologies for communal discernment and participatory planning are increasingly studied and adapted by other religious congregations, dioceses, and faith-based organizations. In the fields of education and social justice, where Jesuits are prominent, this emphasis on listening fosters more collaborative and community-driven initiatives. Educational institutions are encouraged to listen to student needs and societal trends, while social apostolates are tasked with empowering local communities by prioritizing their voices and lived experiences. This approach helps to position the Society of Jesus as a thought leader in organizational adaptation and ethical engagement, demonstrating how large, historically rooted institutions can remain relevant and impactful through genuine attentiveness to internal and external stakeholders. ### Future Outlook The Society of Jesus is poised to continue integrating its listening posture into all facets of its global mission. The completion of the Synod on Synodality is expected to provide further insights and directives that will likely shape the next phase of Jesuit engagement. Future efforts will likely focus on deepening the practice of spiritual conversation and discernment within Jesuit communities and their ministries, ensuring that listening is not a one-time event but an ongoing habit of the heart and mind. The challenge will be to maintain this commitment amidst the pressures of global events and the demands of their diverse apostolates. As the world continues to grapple with climate change, social inequality, and political fragmentation, the Jesuits' capacity for discerning listening will be crucial in identifying areas where their unique charism can offer the most impactful service. ### Conclusion The Society of Jesus's commitment to 'listening to the world speaking' is more than a strategy; it is a re-articulation of its foundational Ignatian spirituality for the contemporary age. Through the Universal Apostolic Preferences and active participation in the Synod on Synodality, the Jesuits are demonstrating a renewed dedication to discerning God's presence in the complexities of human experience. This deliberate posture of attentiveness aims to ensure that the Jesuit mission remains vibrant, relevant, and deeply connected to the needs and aspirations of humanity. As the world navigates profound changes, the Society of Jesus seeks to lead by example, proving that profound engagement begins with the humble, yet powerful, act of truly listening.
