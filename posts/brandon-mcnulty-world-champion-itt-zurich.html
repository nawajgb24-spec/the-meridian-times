McNulty Ends 34-Year Drought, Claims Historic World Time Trial Title in Zurich

Brandon McNulty made history at the 2024 UCI Road World Championships in Zurich, becoming the first American male elite road cycling world champion since Greg LeMond's 1990 triumph. His stunning victory in the Individual Time Trial ended a 34-year wait, marking a significant moment for US cycling and cementing his place among the sport's elite.

Zurich, Switzerland – “I guess miracles happen,” remarked a disbelieving Brandon McNulty, moments after clinching the rainbow jersey in the Individual Time Trial at the 2024 UCI Road World Championships in Zurich. His victory on September 22, 2024, was not merely a personal triumph but a seismic event for American cycling, ending a 34-year drought for a male elite world champion on the road. The 26-year-old from Phoenix, Arizona, outpaced a formidable field of time trial specialists, delivering a performance that combined power, precision, and an unyielding will. This win marks the first time an American male cyclist has stood atop the podium at a senior road world championship event since Greg LeMond’s iconic Road Race triumph in 1990, ushering in a new era of optimism for USA Cycling. **Background** Brandon McNulty's journey to the pinnacle of time trialing has been a steady ascent marked by early promise and consistent development. Born on April 2, 1998, McNulty quickly established himself as a prodigious talent in the junior ranks, particularly against the clock. His potential was globally recognized in 2016 when he secured the Junior Individual Time Trial World Championship title. This early success set a clear trajectory for his career, signaling his innate ability to excel in one of cycling's most demanding disciplines. After turning professional in 2017, McNulty progressively moved through the ranks, first with Rally Cycling and then joining the WorldTour powerhouse UAE Team Emirates in 2020. With the Emirati squad, he has evolved from a promising domestique into a crucial all-rounder capable of strong Grand Tour performances and, increasingly, standout time trial results. His career has been characterized by steady progress. He has consistently placed well in various stage races, often contributing significantly to his team leaders' successes while also seizing opportunities for individual glory. His performances in the Olympic Games, Grand Tours like the Giro d'Italia and Tour de France, and major one-day races have showcased his versatility and growing strength. **Timeline** **1990:** Greg LeMond wins the UCI Road World Championship Road Race, becoming the last American male elite road cycling world champion for over three decades. **2016:** Brandon McNulty dominates the Junior Individual Time Trial at the UCI Road World Championships in Doha, Qatar, securing his first rainbow jersey. **2017:** McNulty begins his professional career, continuing his development as a time trialist and stage race contender. **2020:** Joins UAE Team Emirates, marking his entry into the elite WorldTour level. Makes his Grand Tour debut at the Giro d'Italia. **2021:** Represents the United States at the Tokyo Olympic Games, competing in both the Individual Time Trial and Road Race, gaining crucial experience on the global stage. **2022-2023:** Demonstrates increasing strength in time trials and mountainous terrain, securing notable results in high-profile stage races and Grand Tour stages, confirming his status as a top-tier professional. **September 22, 2024:** Brandon McNulty starts the Elite Men's Individual Time Trial at the UCI Road World Championships in Zurich, Switzerland. He delivers a career-defining ride, crossing the finish line with the fastest time, ultimately securing the gold medal and the coveted rainbow jersey. **Latest Developments** McNulty’s performance in Zurich was a masterclass in pacing and technical execution. The 46-kilometer course, known for its undulating profile and challenging turns through the city and surrounding areas, demanded both raw power and acute bike-handling skills. From an early start, McNulty set a blistering pace, consistently registering among the fastest split times. He navigated the course’s steep climbs and rapid descents with an intensity that hinted at something special. As the final few powerhouses, including Belgian sensation Wout van Aert and reigning Italian champion Filippo Ganna, completed their efforts, McNulty’s time held firm. The tension was palpable in the finish area as each rider's final seconds ticked away, culminating in the realization that McNulty's exceptional ride could not be beaten. The official results confirmed McNulty’s victory, with Van Aert taking silver and Ganna claiming bronze. His winning margin, though not vast, underscored the dominance of his performance on a day where precision and tenacity were paramount. The immediate scenes of celebration, mixed with McNulty's humble disbelief, quickly went viral across social media platforms. **Analysis** McNulty’s victory is significant for several reasons. Primarily, it shatters a long-standing psychological barrier for American male cyclists. For decades, the shadow of Greg LeMond's unparalleled success loomed large, making any subsequent elite world title seem an almost insurmountable challenge. His win in the Individual Time Trial speaks to the meticulous preparation and strategic development McNulty and his team, UAE Team Emirates, have invested in. While often viewed as a pure power event, modern time trialing is also a tactical chess match, demanding aerodynamic efficiency, precise pacing, and mental fortitude—all qualities McNulty showcased in Zurich. This achievement also highlights the ongoing evolution of McNulty as a rider. From a junior prodigy, he has matured into a consistent performer across various terrains and disciplines. His ability to perform at peak level under immense pressure against the world's best signals a new phase in his career, positioning him as a legitimate threat in future major championships and Grand Tours. **Public Response** The news of McNulty’s victory reverberated widely, generating an outpouring of excitement and national pride. Social media platforms were awash with congratulatory messages from fans, fellow athletes, and former champions. USA Cycling issued a statement celebrating the historic achievement, emphasizing its significance for the sport's growth in the country. Many in the cycling community expressed a sense of relief and optimism, viewing McNulty’s rainbow jersey as a testament to the perseverance of American talent. Comparisons to LeMond, while inevitable, were framed with respect for McNulty's unique accomplishment in a different discipline, highlighting his individual journey rather than merely fulfilling a legacy. His humble reaction to the win, captured by the quote “‘I guess miracles happen’,” resonated with the public, adding a layer of relatable humanity to his extraordinary athletic feat. This genuine display of emotion further endeared him to a growing fan base. **Industry Impact** McNulty’s world championship title is expected to have a tangible impact on the cycling industry, particularly within the United States. Such a high-profile win can significantly boost the sport's visibility, attracting new participants and reigniting interest among lapsed fans. For USA Cycling, the victory provides a powerful narrative for sponsorship acquisition and youth development programs. It demonstrates that the pathway from junior talent to elite world champion is viable, potentially inspiring a new generation of American cyclists to pursue the sport seriously. Brands associated with cycling, both within the U.S. and internationally, may see increased engagement and sales. Furthermore, the success of an American rider on the global stage can strengthen the financial backing for domestic professional teams and infrastructure, contributing to a healthier and more robust cycling ecosystem in the U.S. It validates investments made in athlete development and technological advancements. **Future Outlook** Looking ahead, Brandon McNulty's world title undoubtedly elevates his status within the professional peloton. He will wear the coveted rainbow stripes for the next year, a symbol that commands respect and marks him as a rider of exceptional caliber. This will bring increased scrutiny but also new opportunities. His focus will likely remain on multi-stage races and Grand Tours, where his time trial prowess combined with his improving climbing ability makes him a strong general classification contender. The 2025 season will see him as a marked man, but also one with the confidence of a world champion. His performance will also be closely watched for the upcoming Olympic Games, where he will be a strong medal hopeful in the Individual Time Trial. For American cycling, McNulty’s victory serves as a beacon. It signals that after years of rebuilding and nurturing talent, the U.S. is once again capable of producing champions at the highest level. This momentum is crucial for the sport's continued growth and aspirations on the international stage. **Conclusion** Brandon McNulty's triumph in Zurich transcends a mere medal; it is a moment etched into the annals of American cycling history. His modest exclamation, “‘I guess miracles happen’,” belies the years of dedication, sacrifice, and relentless effort that culminated in this extraordinary achievement. By breaking a generational drought, McNulty has not only secured his place among cycling's elite but has also reignited hope and inspiration for an entire nation. His rainbow jersey is a powerful symbol of what is possible, promising a vibrant future for American cycling and ensuring that the legacy of U.S. champions continues to evolve.
