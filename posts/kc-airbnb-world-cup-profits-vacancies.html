In a market with highly elastic demand, particularly during unique, high-profile events, suppliers (hosts) can command premium prices. The World Cup scenario in Kansas City suggests that hosts collectively determined that the marginal revenue gained from booking an additional unit at a lower price did not outweigh the higher average revenue per booking achieved at elevated rates, even if it meant some units remained unbooked. This approach aligns with typical patterns seen in other major event cities where initial price surges may deter some budget-conscious travelers, leaving a portion of the inventory available. However, for those willing to pay premium rates, the market readily supplied options, ensuring robust overall financial performance for the sector. The success of this strategy highlights the strength of demand elasticity during such events. Furthermore, the quality and location of available units also play a crucial role. Premium properties in desirable locations likely achieved higher occupancy rates at peak prices, while less appealing or more remote options might have contributed to the vacant inventory, even as their prices also increased. ### Public Response The public response to the short-term rental market's performance during the World Cup has been varied.
