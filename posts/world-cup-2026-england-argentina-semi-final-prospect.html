Their encounters on the world stage have consistently produced some of the most talked-about matches in World Cup history, cementing a profound and often fiery dynamic between the two nations. The rivalry truly intensified in the 1966 World Cup quarter-finals, hosted by England, where the home side secured a 1-0 victory. This match was marred by controversy surrounding the sending-off of Argentine captain Antonio Rattín, further fueling animosity between the two footballing cultures. Decades later, the 1986 World Cup quarter-final in Mexico City etched itself into folklore. Argentina, led by Diego Maradona, triumphed 2-1. Maradona's infamous 'Hand of God' goal, immediately followed by his sublime 'Goal of the Century,' underscored the complexity and sheer drama of their rivalry. England's Gary Lineker scored a late consolation, but Argentina advanced and ultimately won the tournament. The 1998 World Cup in France saw another classic, with Argentina prevailing over England in the Round of 16 after a dramatic penalty shootout, following a 2-2 draw. David Beckham's red card for a retaliatory kick added another layer to the narrative. Four years later, at the 2002 World Cup in Japan and South Korea, England gained a measure of revenge, winning their group stage encounter 1-0 courtesy of a David Beckham penalty. ### Timeline The roadmap to the FIFA World Cup 2026 has been unfolding over several years. This expansion guarantees more matches and potentially more opportunities for high-stakes encounters deeper into the tournament. Qualification campaigns for the 2026 World Cup commenced in September 2023 with the CONMEBOL (South American) region beginning its league format.
