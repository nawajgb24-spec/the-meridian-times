Warhammer: The Old World Expands Army Roster with New 'Battle March' Releases

Games Workshop continues to expand the faction roster for its recently launched fantasy wargame, Warhammer: The Old World. Following the initial release of Bretonnia and Tomb Kings of Khemri, the company has begun rolling out 'Battle March' armies for additional iconic factions. These comprehensive army sets aim to provide players with substantial forces, signaling a sustained commitment to developing the game's diverse miniature range and catering to a broad base of hobbyists eager to return to or discover the classic setting.

### Introduction Games Workshop, the global miniatures wargaming manufacturer, is systematically broadening the available armies for its flagship fantasy game, Warhammer: The Old World. Since its highly anticipated launch earlier this year, the company has initiated a structured release schedule, introducing 'Battle March' armies for several additional iconic factions. This ongoing expansion is crucial for establishing the game's long-term viability and attracting a wider player base. These 'Battle March' sets typically offer a substantial collection of miniatures, often encompassing core units, command elements, and unique faction choices. The strategy reflects Games Workshop's commitment to providing comprehensive starting points for players, allowing them to rapidly assemble a force and engage with the game's strategic depth and rich lore. ### Background Warhammer: The Old World represents a significant revival for Games Workshop, bringing back the classic fantasy setting that was replaced by Warhammer Age of Sigmar in 2015. Announced in 2019, the project garnered substantial interest from veteran players who yearned for the return of square-based rank-and-file combat and the distinct aesthetic of the Old World. The game is set centuries before the cataclysmic 'End Times' event, offering a fresh narrative foundation. The game officially launched in early 2024, spearheaded by two foundational armies: the chivalrous Bretonnians and the undead legions of the Tomb Kings of Khemri. These initial releases included comprehensive army books, core rules, and a range of new and re-released miniatures, setting the stage for the game's broader development. ### Timeline Warhammer: The Old World's journey began with its announcement in 2019, confirming the return to the classic setting. Following several years of development updates and sneak peeks, the game had its official launch in early 2024. This initial phase saw the release of the core rulebook, along with the first two full army ranges for Bretonnia and Tomb Kings of Khemri, complete with their respective Arcane Journals and model kits. Throughout the subsequent months of 2024, Games Workshop began to unveil and release the first wave of 'Battle March' armies for other factions. This included the Dwarfen Mountain Holds, bringing sturdy infantry and war machines back to battlefields. Shortly thereafter, the menacing Warriors of Chaos and the unruly Orc & Goblin Tribes received their dedicated 'Battle March' sets, expanding the chaotic and greenskin forces available to players. Mid-year further announcements detailed forthcoming releases for factions such as the High Elves, known for their disciplined ranks and magical prowess, and the enigmatic Wood Elves with their forest guardians. The Empire of Man, a cornerstone human faction, and the bestial Beastmen also featured prominently in the ongoing rollout plans, indicating a sustained release schedule extending through the latter half of the year and beyond. ### Latest Developments The most recent announcements from Games Workshop confirm the steady progression of the 'Battle March' army releases. Dedicated army boxes for factions such as the High Elves and Wood Elves are now either available or slated for imminent release. These sets are designed to facilitate entry into the game for new players and provide substantial reinforcements for existing hobbyists. Each 'Battle March' release typically includes a carefully curated selection of units to form a balanced force. For example, a set might contain a hero, multiple regiments of core infantry, and a specialized unit or monstrous creature. These releases are often accompanied by reprints of older, iconic plastic kits and, in some cases, entirely new models or updated resin kits from Forge World, Games Workshop's specialist resin model division, ensuring a blend of classic and contemporary offerings. ### Analysis Games Workshop's measured approach to releasing 'Battle March' armies for Warhammer: The Old World appears to be a strategic decision aimed at cultivating a robust and enduring player base. By staggering the release of factions, the company can maintain consistent engagement within the community and manage production logistics effectively. This method contrasts with a single, large-scale launch, which could overwhelm hobbyists and potentially lead to supply chain issues. This gradual rollout also allows players to invest in the game at a manageable pace, focusing on one or two factions before the full breadth of the setting becomes available. It caters to different segments of the player base: returning veterans can rebuild their favorite armies, while new players have accessible entry points. The emphasis on 'Battle March' boxes simplifies army construction, reducing the initial complexity for new entrants to the system. ### Public Response The public response to the expanding 'Battle March' army range has been largely positive within the Warhammer community. Players have expressed enthusiasm for the return of long-absent factions and the opportunity to field beloved armies once again. The quality of both newly sculpted models and carefully curated re-releases has generally met with approval, satisfying long-standing expectations. However, some discussions persist regarding the pace of releases and the overall cost associated with building a full army, a common consideration within the miniature wargaming hobby. While the 'Battle March' boxes offer perceived value as starting points, the comprehensive investment required for larger forces remains a topic of community discussion. Availability of specific kits, especially older resin or metal models, has also been a point of interest, with some players anticipating further re-releases or plastic updates. ### Industry Impact The re-introduction and sustained development of Warhammer: The Old World, propelled by its expanding 'Battle March' releases, has created ripples across the wargaming industry. It reinforces Games Workshop's dominant position by leveraging a highly nostalgic and iconic intellectual property. The game directly appeals to a demographic of players who may have left the hobby or transitioned to other systems after Warhammer Fantasy Battle's discontinuation. Its success could also influence other wargame manufacturers to revisit classic settings or introduce new games with a focus on traditional rank-and-file combat. While Warhammer Age of Sigmar remains Games Workshop's primary fantasy offering, The Old World effectively recaptures a segment of the market with distinct preferences, demonstrating the viability of catering to diverse player tastes within a single company's portfolio. The release strategy further showcases a model for revitalizing legacy game lines. ### Future Outlook The future outlook for Warhammer: The Old World, supported by the continuous stream of 'Battle March' armies, appears stable. Games Workshop has a deep well of factions and lore to draw from within the Old World setting, suggesting many more army releases are planned beyond the current wave. Players can anticipate further expansions, including additional unit types, unique characters, and potential sub-faction variations for already released armies. Beyond model releases, it is reasonable to expect further Arcane Journals, which provide detailed rules and lore for each faction, as well as potential campaign supplements that expand the game's narrative elements. The long-term strategy likely involves a careful balance between introducing new content and maintaining the foundational appeal that initially attracted players to the Old World. ### Conclusion The ongoing release of 'Battle March' armies is a critical component of Games Workshop's strategy for Warhammer: The Old World. By consistently introducing new factions and expanding existing ranges, the company is ensuring the game's vitality and appeal. This measured rollout not only taps into significant player nostalgia but also provides accessible entry points for newcomers, securing the game's position as a prominent force in the fantasy wargaming landscape. The commitment to a broad and diverse army roster suggests a sustained effort to build a thriving community around this resurrected classic.
