UNO Faculty Earn Global Top 2% Researcher Distinction

Thirteen faculty members from the University of Nebraska Omaha (UNO) have been named among the top 2% of scientists worldwide, according to a prestigious database compiled by Stanford University and published by Elsevier. This recognition highlights the significant and far-reaching impact of UNO's research across diverse academic fields. The ranking, based on comprehensive citation metrics, positions UNO as a key contributor to global knowledge advancement, enhancing its reputation in the international research community.

### Introduction The University of Nebraska Omaha (UNO) has received significant international recognition, with thirteen of its faculty members identified as being among the top 2% of scientists globally. This esteemed acknowledgment comes from a comprehensive, publicly available database compiled by researchers at Stanford University and published by Elsevier. The inclusion of such a substantial number of UNO academics underscores the university's growing influence in the global research landscape and the profound impact of its scholarly contributions. This distinction is based on standardized citation metrics, evaluating the career-long or single-year research output of millions of scientists worldwide. The announcement positions UNO's faculty alongside leading minds across various disciplines, affirming the institution's commitment to pioneering research and academic excellence. The recognition is expected to enhance UNO's profile, attracting further collaboration, funding, and talent. ### Background The 'Top 2% of World’s Scientists' database is a highly regarded benchmark in the academic community, offering a standardized measure of research impact. Initiated by Dr. John Ioannidis and his team at Stanford University, the methodology for this comprehensive list was developed to provide transparent and objective insights into the scientific contributions of researchers across the globe. It leverages data from Elsevier's Scopus, one of the largest abstract and citation databases of peer-reviewed literature. The ranking system employs a composite indicator that considers several key metrics, including the total number of citations, the h-index (a metric that quantifies an individual's scientific research output), co-authorship, and a measure of impact through a field-specific percentile rank. Researchers are categorized into 22 major scientific fields and 176 sub-fields, ensuring a nuanced comparison within specific areas of expertise. This rigorous approach aims to mitigate biases often present in simple citation counts, providing a more holistic view of a researcher's influence. ### Timeline The development of the initial methodology for this comprehensive database dates back several years, with ongoing refinements to ensure accuracy and relevance. The database itself is dynamic, undergoing annual updates to reflect the latest citation data and research advancements. Typically, these updates involve processing millions of publication records and their associated citations, with the most recent iteration incorporating data up to the preceding calendar year. Following the publication of the updated database, institutions globally identify their faculty members who meet the stringent criteria for inclusion. The University of Nebraska Omaha's announcement of its thirteen faculty members' achievement aligns with this annual cycle, signifying their sustained and impactful contributions within the period covered by the latest data release. While specific past dates of UNO faculty inclusion are not uniformly publicized, this latest cohort demonstrates a continuous commitment to research excellence at the institution. ### Latest Developments The current recognition highlights thirteen distinct faculty members from UNO, whose research spans a diverse array of academic disciplines.
