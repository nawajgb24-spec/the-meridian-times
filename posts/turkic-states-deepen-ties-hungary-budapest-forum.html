OTS Secretary General Strengthens Turkic-Hungarian Ties in Budapest

The Secretary General of the Organization of Turkic States (OTS), Kubanychbek Omuraliev, recently concluded a significant visit to Budapest, Hungary. The visit, marked by his participation in the OTS Business Forum and high-level meetings with Prime Minister Viktor Orbán and Foreign Minister Péter Szijjártó, underscored the growing strategic alignment between the Turkic World and Central Europe. Discussions focused on deepening economic cooperation, expanding trade, and enhancing connectivity, further solidifying Hungary’s role as an observer state within the OTS framework and advancing its 'Eastern Opening' policy.

### Introduction Budapest, Hungary – The Organization of Turkic States (OTS) Secretary General, Kubanychbek Omuraliev, undertook a pivotal visit to Hungary in late November 2023, reinforcing the country's strategic position within the Turkic World. The visit encompassed active participation in the OTS Business Forum and significant bilateral engagements with Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán and Minister of Foreign Affairs and Trade Péter Szijjártó. These events highlighted a concerted effort to foster closer political, economic, and cultural ties between Turkic nations and Hungary. Omuraliev's agenda focused on exploring new avenues for collaboration, particularly in trade, investment, and transport. The interactions emphasized the mutual benefits of a strengthened partnership, aiming to leverage Hungary's 'Eastern Opening' policy and the collective potential of the Turkic states for regional stability and economic growth. ### Background The Organization of Turkic States, formerly known as the Turkic Council, serves as an international intergovernmental organization encompassing independent Turkic countries. Its primary objectives include promoting comprehensive cooperation among its member states in political, economic, security, and cultural spheres. Member states currently include Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Turkey, and Uzbekistan. Hungary's relationship with the Turkic cooperation framework deepened significantly when it obtained observer status in the Turkic Council in 2018. This move aligned with Hungary's 'Eastern Opening' foreign policy, a strategic initiative launched in 2010 to diversify its economic and political partnerships beyond traditional Western European alliances, focusing on dynamic economies in the East, particularly in Central Asia and the Turkic regions. Historical and cultural affinities also underpin Hungary's engagement with the Turkic World. Many Hungarians trace their origins to ancient Turkic tribes, a connection often highlighted in diplomatic discourse. This shared heritage provides a cultural foundation for political and economic rapprochement, distinguishing Hungary's approach from other European nations. ### Timeline **2009:** The Nakhchivan Agreement establishes the Turkic Council, marking the formal institutionalization of cooperation among Turkic-speaking states. **2010:** Hungary initiates its 'Eastern Opening' foreign policy, actively seeking stronger ties with countries in the East, including Turkic states. **2018:** Hungary is granted observer status in the Turkic Council during the Sixth Summit of the Turkic Council in Cholpon-Ata, Kyrgyzstan. This marks a formal integration into the Turkic cooperation framework. **2019:** The Hungarian representation office of the Turkic Council is inaugurated in Budapest, further cementing its engagement. **November 2021:** At its 8th Summit in Istanbul, the Turkic Council is officially renamed the Organization of Turkic States (OTS), reflecting an expanded scope and ambition for integration. **Late November 2023:** OTS Secretary General Kubanychbek Omuraliev visits Budapest, participating in the OTS Business Forum and engaging in high-level bilateral meetings with Hungarian leadership. ### Latest Developments Secretary General Omuraliev's late November 2023 visit to Budapest was dominated by his participation in the OTS Business Forum, held on November 28-29. This forum convened business leaders, investors, and government representatives from OTS member and observer states, including a strong delegation from Hungary. The Business Forum served as a crucial platform to identify and facilitate concrete investment and trade opportunities. Discussions ranged from enhancing logistics and transport corridors – particularly the Middle Corridor connecting Asia and Europe – to fostering cooperation in energy security, agriculture, and digital transformation. The potential for joint ventures and cross-border investments was a central theme. Beyond the forum, Omuraliev held separate, in-depth discussions with Prime Minister Viktor Orbán. The leaders reaffirmed their commitment to deepening bilateral relations and strengthening cooperation within the OTS. Prime Minister Orbán reiterated Hungary's strong support for the OTS's agenda and its desire to contribute actively to its initiatives. Similarly, the meeting with Foreign Minister Péter Szijjártó focused on the geopolitical implications of enhanced Turkic-Hungarian cooperation. Szijjártó emphasized Hungary's strategic interest in maintaining stable and diverse partnerships, seeing the Turkic states as key partners in an evolving global landscape. Both sides stressed the importance of parliamentary diplomacy and cultural exchanges as supplementary tools for fostering closer ties. ### Analysis The recent events in Budapest signify a maturing relationship between Hungary and the Organization of Turkic States. For Hungary, this engagement offers a strategic pathway to diversify its foreign policy and economic dependencies. By strengthening ties with the dynamic economies of the Turkic World, Hungary aims to enhance its economic resilience, particularly in areas like energy supply and trade routes, reducing reliance on single sources or corridors. From the perspective of the OTS, Hungary’s active observer status provides a crucial bridge to Central Europe and, by extension, the European Union. Hungary's advocacy within European forums can help raise the profile of the Turkic World and foster greater understanding and cooperation between the two regions. This expansion of influence beyond its immediate geographic footprint is a significant strategic gain for the OTS. The emphasis on the Middle Corridor, a trans-Caspian international transport route, is particularly noteworthy. As global supply chains continue to seek alternative routes, cooperation between Hungary and the Turkic states on this corridor holds immense potential for increasing trade flows and connectivity between Asia and Europe, benefiting all involved parties. ### Public Response Official statements from both Hungarian and OTS leadership have consistently framed these developments in a positive light, highlighting the mutual benefits of collaboration. Hungarian government officials frequently underscore the historical and cultural ties, alongside the economic advantages of the 'Eastern Opening' policy. The narrative often focuses on pragmatic cooperation and shared interests. Within the Turkic states, Hungary's active participation is generally welcomed as a sign of growing international recognition and influence for the OTS. News coverage in state-affiliated media in these countries tends to emphasize the expanding network of partnerships and the potential for collective economic prosperity. Public discourse, primarily driven by official channels, reflects a supportive stance towards strengthening these cross-regional bonds. Specific, independent public opinion polling on these precise events is not widely available, but the overall governmental messaging suggests a positive reception. ### Industry Impact The OTS Business Forum and the ensuing high-level discussions are poised to have tangible impacts across several industries. The focus on enhancing the Middle Corridor directly benefits logistics, transport, and infrastructure sectors, potentially leading to new investments in rail, road, and port development across Central Asia and the Caucasus, extending into Europe via Hungary. Energy cooperation, particularly in diversifying supply routes and sources, could see increased partnerships between Hungarian energy companies and those in Turkic states rich in hydrocarbons. The agricultural sector is also a prime area for collaboration, with opportunities for technology transfer, investment in food processing, and expanded trade in agricultural products. Furthermore, the Turkic Investment Fund, established to mobilize capital for joint projects, is expected to play a vital role in financing these initiatives. Its operationalization will facilitate greater cross-border investment, benefiting sectors from manufacturing to tourism by providing necessary financial backing and reducing investment risks. This fund is crucial for translating discussions into concrete economic projects. ### Future Outlook The visit of Secretary General Omuraliev to Hungary signals a trajectory of deepening engagement. Future cooperation is expected to expand beyond traditional economic and political spheres to include areas such as education, science, technology, and youth exchange programs. Hungary's ongoing commitment as an observer state could potentially evolve, though its current status allows for significant influence without full membership obligations. Challenges remain, including navigating complex geopolitical dynamics, ensuring seamless implementation of agreements, and addressing potential bureaucratic hurdles. However, the strong political will demonstrated by both sides suggests a robust foundation for overcoming these obstacles. Regular high-level meetings and working group sessions are anticipated to maintain momentum and translate strategic visions into practical outcomes. ### Conclusion The recent visit by the OTS Secretary General to Hungary represents a significant chapter in the ongoing narrative of cooperation between the Turkic World and Central Europe. Through the OTS Business Forum and strategic bilateral meetings, both sides reaffirmed their commitment to fostering a stronger, more integrated partnership. The emphasis on economic cooperation, connectivity, and cultural exchange positions this relationship as a key component in regional development and diversification for all involved. As Hungary continues its 'Eastern Opening' and the OTS consolidates its regional influence, the synergy forged in Budapest is set to yield long-term benefits across economic and geopolitical landscapes.
