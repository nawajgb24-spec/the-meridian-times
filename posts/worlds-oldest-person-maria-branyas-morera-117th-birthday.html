Maria Branyas Morera Celebrates 117th Birthday, Cementing Status as World's Oldest Person

Maria Branyas Morera, officially recognized as the world's oldest living person, marked her 117th birthday on March 4, 2024. Born in the United States and residing in Spain since childhood, her remarkable longevity has seen her live through over a century of global events, from two World Wars to the recent COVID-19 pandemic. Her milestone birthday draws international attention to the science of extreme human lifespan and the unique insights her life offers into the factors contributing to supercentenarian status.

Maria Branyas Morera, officially recognized by Guinness World Records as the world’s oldest living person, marked a monumental milestone on March 4, 2024, celebrating her 117th birthday. Residing in a care home in Olot, Catalonia, Spain, her extraordinary life has spanned over a century of global transformations, witnessing two World Wars, the Spanish Civil War, and the 1918 Spanish Flu pandemic, ultimately surviving the more recent COVID-19 pandemic. Her continued presence as a supercentenarian not only captivates public imagination but also offers invaluable data for scientific research into the furthest reaches of human longevity. Born in San Francisco, California, in 1907, Maria Branyas Morera was the daughter of Spanish immigrants. Her family returned to Spain when she was eight years old, settling in Catalonia. From an early age, she experienced significant historical upheaval. Her childhood was marked by the global impact of the Spanish Flu, a pandemic that claimed millions of lives worldwide. Later, as an adult, she navigated the tumultuous period of the Spanish Civil War (1936-1939) and the subsequent decades of post-war recovery and social change in Europe. She married Joan Moret, a physician, and together they raised three children. She has since outlived her husband and one of her children, and is a proud grandmother to 11 and great-grandmother to 13, highlighting the deep familial roots that have anchored her long life. For the past 23 years, she has resided at the Santa María del Tura care home, where she continues to live a largely tranquil existence, albeit with the natural challenges that accompany extreme old age.
