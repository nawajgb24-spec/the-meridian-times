Architect Jim Craig on Building Rodeo Dunes' 'World's Largest Himalayas-Style Putting Green'

Architect Jim Craig is at the helm of creating what is touted as the world's largest Himalayas-style putting green at Rodeo Dunes, Mike Keiser's ambitious new golf development in northeast Colorado. Drawing inspiration from the iconic St. Andrews putting course, Craig's design for 'The Rodeo' aims to offer a vast, dynamic, and accessible short-game experience, integral to Keiser's philosophy of fun, natural golf. This article delves into the project's unique challenges, design ethos, and its anticipated impact on golf tourism and course design.

### Introduction In the burgeoning landscape of destination golf, a new venture in northeast Colorado is capturing significant attention. Rodeo Dunes, the latest development from golf impresario Mike Keiser, is set to introduce a distinctive element: a vast, undulating putting course designed by architect Jim Craig. This facility, unofficially dubbed 'The Rodeo,' aims to emulate, and potentially exceed in scale, the famous Himalayas putting green at St. Andrews, Scotland, making it one of the largest of its kind globally. The project’s ambition extends beyond mere size, focusing on creating a unique, engaging, and accessible golf experience. Jim Craig, an architect with a track record of working on Keiser’s minimalist, natural designs, is tasked with translating this vision into a tangible reality. The putting green is envisioned as a central social hub and an entertaining challenge for golfers of all abilities. ### Background Rodeo Dunes is emerging in the plains east of Denver, near Commerce City, Colorado. It represents Mike Keiser’s continued commitment to developing world-class, walkable golf courses in natural, often remote, settings. Keiser’s portfolio includes highly acclaimed resorts such as Bandon Dunes, Sand Valley, and Cabot Links, all celebrated for their natural beauty and pure golf experiences. The concept of a Himalayas-style putting course is rooted in the tradition of informal, fun-oriented golf. The original Himalayas at St. Andrews is an ancient, sprawling putting green known for its dramatic contours, bumps, and swales, offering a casual yet challenging experience. Keiser’s decision to incorporate such a feature at Rodeo Dunes underscores his belief in golf's social and recreational dimensions, alongside its competitive aspects. Colorado's high plains, with their sandy soil and subtle natural undulations, provide an ideal canvas for this type of golf architecture. The terrain facilitates the creation of dramatic features without extensive earthmoving, aligning perfectly with the minimalist, 'land-form' golf design philosophy favored by Keiser and his chosen architects. ### Timeline The Rodeo Dunes project has been in development for several years, following Keiser's acquisition of the land and the formal announcement of the golf resort. Initial architectural planning and site work began in earnest following these announcements. Construction of the main course and ancillary facilities, including the putting green, has been progressing steadily. While specific construction milestones for 'The Rodeo' are not always publicly detailed, its development runs concurrently with the larger resort. Anticipation is building for the resort's full opening, which is currently projected for 2025. However, there are expectations that limited preview play opportunities, potentially including access to the expansive putting green, could become available as early as late 2024. ### Latest Developments Recent updates from the Rodeo Dunes site confirm substantial progress on 'The Rodeo' putting green. Architect Jim Craig has indicated that the design emphasizes natural flow and varied challenges, aiming for a putting surface that encourages creativity and strategic thinking rather than just simple hole-outs. Craig's approach reportedly involves shaping the contours to offer multiple pathways to each hole, ensuring that every round on the expansive green presents a fresh set of puzzles. This philosophy prioritizes repeat playability and encourages groups to invent their own games and competitions. Details emerging from the design process highlight the integration of the putting course with the broader resort environment. It is envisioned not just as a standalone attraction but as a seamless extension of the golf experience, inviting guests to unwind or engage in friendly competition before or after their rounds on the main course. ### Analysis Building a putting green of this magnitude presents unique architectural and maintenance challenges. Jim Craig's expertise in working with natural landscapes and Keiser's proven development model are critical assets. The design must balance spectacle with playability, ensuring that its immense scale doesn't overwhelm the user experience. The 'Himalayas-style' designation implies a deliberate emphasis on dramatic elevation changes, hidden breaks, and unpredictable bounces. Craig's vision for 'The Rodeo' appears to be one that embraces these elements, translating them into a contemporary context while retaining the spirit of its St. Andrews namesake. Successful execution will hinge on the nuanced shaping of the green's surface, the strategic placement of holes, and the thoughtful integration of its surroundings. The goal is to create an immersive, almost miniature golf course experience, but one designed with the precision and artistry of a championship layout. ### Public Response The announcement of Rodeo Dunes, particularly the inclusion of an ambitious Himalayas-style putting green, has generated considerable excitement within the golf community. Enthusiasts and industry insiders are keenly anticipating how this unique feature will complement Keiser's other renowned properties. Early reactions suggest a strong positive reception, with many praising Keiser's continued innovation and commitment to accessible, fun golf. The prospect of 'the world's largest' putting green has created a significant buzz, drawing attention not only to Rodeo Dunes but also to the broader trend of enhancing short-game facilities at golf resorts. Local Colorado golfers and residents have also expressed enthusiasm for the new development, recognizing its potential to boost tourism and provide another top-tier golf destination within the state. The unique nature of 'The Rodeo' is seen as a major draw. ### Industry Impact Rodeo Dunes and its colossal putting green are poised to make a notable impact on the golf industry. Keiser's projects often set benchmarks for design, sustainability, and visitor experience. 'The Rodeo' could inspire other developers to invest more significantly in creative, large-scale short-game facilities. This trend reinforces the growing recognition of golf's social and recreational aspects, moving beyond the traditional 18-hole round. Putting courses, par-3 courses, and expansive practice areas are becoming integral to attracting a wider audience and catering to diverse skill levels and time constraints. Architecturally, the project highlights the ongoing evolution of golf course design, where creativity with natural landforms and the prioritization of player enjoyment are paramount. Jim Craig's work on this scale could further cement the importance of innovative short-game design as a critical component of modern golf resorts. ### Future Outlook Upon its anticipated opening, 'The Rodeo' putting green is expected to become a signature attraction at Rodeo Dunes. It will serve as a dynamic gathering place, offering both a casual pastime and a spirited competitive venue. Its success could further solidify Rodeo Dunes' reputation as a must-visit destination, drawing golfers from across the globe seeking a unique and memorable experience. The integration of this feature with Keiser's traditional, highly acclaimed main courses suggests a comprehensive, multi-faceted golf offering. Looking ahead, 'The Rodeo' is likely to become a model for future golf developments, demonstrating that innovative, large-scale short-game facilities can significantly enhance the overall appeal and profitability of a golf resort. Its legacy will be measured not just in its size, but in the joy and camaraderie it fosters among players. ### Conclusion Jim Craig's architectural vision for the Himalayas-style putting green at Rodeo Dunes represents a significant undertaking in contemporary golf design. As one of the world's largest, 'The Rodeo' is more than just a novelty; it is a meticulously planned experience that embodies the spirit of fun, accessible, and engaging golf championed by Mike Keiser. Its development signals a continued evolution in how golf resorts approach player experience, cementing the idea that the short game can deliver long-lasting enjoyment and foster a vibrant social atmosphere at the heart of a world-class destination.
