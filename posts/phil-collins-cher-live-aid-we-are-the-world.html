Phil Collins Recalls Cher Taking His Live Aid 'We Are the World' Line

Phil Collins has recently detailed a unique anecdote from the iconic 1985 Live Aid concert, claiming that Cher performed his specific vocal line during the 'We Are the World' finale in Philadelphia after he had departed the stage early. Collins, famous for his unprecedented transatlantic journey to perform at both the London and U.S. legs of the charity event, shared this observation, adding a new layer to the legend of one of music history's most ambitious live performances. The revelation revisits the improvisational spirit of the monumental global concert and the enduring fascination with its behind-the-scenes moments.

## Introduction Phil Collins, the iconic British musician, has recently shed light on a lesser-known moment from the legendary 1985 Live Aid concert, an event etched into the annals of music history. Collins claims that during the star-studded 'We Are the World' finale in Philadelphia, his specific vocal contribution to the charity anthem was performed by fellow artist Cher, a development that occurred after he had made an early exit from the stage. This anecdote, shared by Collins in recent public statements and his autobiography, offers a fresh perspective on the monumental dual-continent concert. It highlights the often-chaotic, yet ultimately successful, improvisational nature of Live Aid, an event remembered for its global solidarity and unprecedented logistical feats. ## Background Live Aid, conceived by Bob Geldof and Midge Ure, was a dual-venue concert held on July 13, 1985, simultaneously at Wembley Stadium in London, UK, and JFK Stadium in Philadelphia, USA. Its primary goal was to raise funds for famine relief in Ethiopia, capturing the world's attention and uniting an extraordinary roster of musical talent. Phil Collins played a distinctive and demanding role in the event. He was the only artist to perform at both concerts, undertaking a transatlantic journey via Concorde. In London, he performed a solo set and drummed for Sting and Eric Clapton. After his flight, he arrived in Philadelphia to perform another solo set and famously drum for the Led Zeppelin reunion, a performance fraught with its own set of challenges. The U.S. leg of Live Aid culminated in a mass performance of 'We Are the World,' the USA for Africa charity single written by Michael Jackson and Lionel Richie. Released earlier in 1985, the song had already become a global phenomenon, featuring dozens of prominent artists. Collins was among the original vocalists on the studio track, singing the distinctive line, “There’s a choice we’re making, we’re saving our own lives.” ## Timeline **January 28, 1985:** The supergroup USA for Africa gathers in Los Angeles to record ‘We Are the World.’ Phil Collins is among the 45 artists who lend their voices to the track, contributing a solo line. **March 7, 1985:** ‘We Are the World’ is released as a single, rapidly ascending to global chart success and becoming one of the best-selling singles of all time, raising millions for African famine relief. **July 13, 1985:** Live Aid concerts unfold. Collins performs at Wembley Stadium, delivering a solo set and drumming for other acts. Immediately afterward, he boards a Concorde flight to Philadelphia. **July 13, 1985 (later in the day):** Collins arrives in Philadelphia, performs a solo set at JFK Stadium, and drums for the highly anticipated Led Zeppelin reunion. The U.S. concert concludes with an ensemble performance of ‘We Are the World.’ **Post-2016:** Phil Collins begins publicly recounting the anecdote, including it in his 2016 autobiography, ‘Not Dead Yet,’ detailing his recollection of Cher stepping in to sing his line during the Philadelphia finale. ## Latest Developments In recent interviews, Phil Collins has reiterated his account of the 'We Are the World' finale in Philadelphia. He recalled the immense fatigue from his unprecedented bi-continental performance schedule, which included hours of travel and multiple sets. This exhaustion reportedly led him to leave the stage slightly early during the chaotic, celebrity-packed ensemble performance of 'We Are the World' at the U.S. concert’s conclusion. Collins specifically noted that his distinctive vocal line, “There’s a choice we’re making, we’re saving our own lives,” was subsequently sung by Cher. Cher, also a prominent figure in the music industry, was among the many stars present on stage for the powerful closing number, a moment of collective musical solidarity. The revelation is not presented by Collins with any sense of grievance or surprise, but rather as a factual observation of the spontaneous nature of such a massive, live charity event. It underscores the challenges of coordinating dozens of global superstars in a single, unscripted moment. ## Analysis Collins's recollection offers a glimpse into the impromptu decisions and minor alterations that can occur during live, unscripted performances of this magnitude. While 'We Are the World' was meticulously recorded in the studio, its live rendition at Live Aid was subject to the presence and availability of artists, the order of performance, and the general flow of a multi-hour concert. The anecdote highlights the unique logistical challenges faced by Collins, who essentially performed two full Live Aid shows across two continents within a single day. His early departure from the stage during the finale in Philadelphia, whether due to fatigue or other logistical reasons, created an immediate need for someone else to fill a crucial vocal part. Cher, a powerful vocalist and a participant in the finale, would have been a natural choice to step in. This small detail, while seemingly minor, contributes to the rich tapestry of Live Aid lore. It reminds audiences that even the most iconic, globally televised events contain human elements, improvisation, and behind-the-scenes adjustments that are not always immediately apparent to viewers. ## Public Response The public response to Phil Collins's anecdote has largely been one of nostalgic interest rather than controversy. The casual nature of Collins's recounting has likely contributed to this reception, positioning the story as a charming historical footnote rather than a critical exposé. ## Industry Impact The revelation from Phil Collins is unlikely to have a major impact on the music industry or the historical perception of Live Aid.
