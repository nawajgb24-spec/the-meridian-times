Their joint application to strike out or achieve summary judgment against a landmark group litigation order (GLO) brought by over 200 former professional and semi-professional players has been rejected. This decision, handed down by Master Lisa Sullivan, means that the extensive lawsuits alleging a failure in duty of care to protect players from permanent brain injury will now proceed to a full trial. The ruling marks a crucial procedural victory for the claimants and intensifies the scrutiny on player welfare in professional rugby. ### Background The lawsuits stem from growing concerns over the long-term neurological health of former rugby players, with many reporting debilitating conditions. The claimants allege that World Rugby, the RFU, and the WRU failed to take reasonable steps to protect them from the risks of repeated head trauma and concussions during their careers. The conditions cited in the legal claims include early-onset dementia, Parkinson's disease, motor neurone disease, and chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE). These conditions are often linked to repetitive head injuries, which are inherent in contact sports like rugby. Former players, many of whom started their careers in the amateur era before the sport went professional, have spoken out about the devastating impact these conditions have had on their lives and their families. Their testimonies have been central to bringing these issues into the public and legal spheres. The legal action seeks damages for negligence, asserting that the governing bodies were aware, or should have been aware, of the significant risks associated with concussions and sub-concussive impacts but did not implement adequate safety measures, protocols, or rule changes. ### Timeline The origins of this legal challenge can be traced back to December 2020, when a group of former international players, including England World Cup winner Steve Thompson, announced their intention to sue the rugby authorities. Their powerful personal accounts highlighted the severity of their conditions and sparked a wider movement. In 2021, formal legal proceedings were initiated, with the number of claimants steadily growing. By April 2022, the High Court recognized the scale and complexity of the claims by granting a Group Litigation Order (GLO), consolidating individual cases into a single action for more efficient judicial management. Throughout 2023, legal teams for World Rugby, the RFU, and the WRU prepared and subsequently filed applications to either strike out the claims entirely or secure a summary judgment.
