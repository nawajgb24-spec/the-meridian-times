Brewers Fan Larry Leapley's Decades-Long Walk to Every Home Game

Larry Leapley, a dedicated Milwaukee Brewers fan, has maintained a remarkable tradition for over 45 years: walking to every home game, including pivotal World Series matchups. From his West Allis home, Leapley covers nine miles round trip, a testament to his unwavering loyalty and personal commitment that began in 1979. His story highlights the profound connection between sports teams and their most devoted supporters, earning him recognition across the fan base and the organization.

### Introduction In the realm of professional sports, where fan engagement often involves digital interaction or short commutes, one Milwaukee Brewers supporter stands apart. Larry Leapley, a resident of West Allis, Wisconsin, has cultivated a unique and enduring tradition: walking to every Brewers home game. This practice, spanning more than four decades, has cemented his status as a legendary figure among the team's faithful. His journey, a daily pilgrimage of approximately 4.5 miles each way, symbolizes a profound commitment to his beloved team. From regular season contests to high-stakes playoff battles, including the 1982 World Series, Leapley's feet have consistently carried him to the stadium gates, rain or shine, through various seasons and Brewers eras. ### Background Larry Leapley's remarkable tradition did not begin as a grand gesture of fan devotion but rather from a pragmatic decision. The year was 1978, and a DUI incident prompted Leapley to re-evaluate his transportation habits. Choosing to forego driving to games, he opted for walking, a choice that quickly evolved into a steadfast routine. By 1979, the walk had become his established mode of transport to every Brewers home game. Beyond the initial necessity, the practice offered benefits of cost savings and physical exercise. What started as a personal resolution gradually transformed into an integral part of his life, deeply intertwining with his passion for baseball and the Milwaukee Brewers. His route takes him from his home in West Allis, a suburb west of Milwaukee, to American Family Field. This journey, typically taking an hour and a half on foot, covers diverse urban and suburban landscapes before culminating at the sprawling stadium complex. For Leapley, it is not merely a means of travel but a ritual that precedes and enhances each game experience. ### Timeline **1978:** Larry Leapley experiences a DUI, prompting his decision to discontinue driving to Brewers games. **1979:** The tradition officially begins, with Leapley walking to every single Milwaukee Brewers home game.
