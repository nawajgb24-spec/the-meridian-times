Financial difficulties quickly led to the team's relocation to Milwaukee in 1970, where it was rebranded as the Brewers, a nod to the city's rich brewing heritage. For nearly three decades, the Brewers competed in the American League, cultivating a loyal following. Their most celebrated season during this era occurred in 1982, when a roster featuring future Hall of Famers Robin Yount and Paul Molitor, alongside formidable sluggers like Gorman Thomas and Ben O'Malley, powered them to the American League pennant. This 'Harvey's Wallbangers' squad faced the St. Louis Cardinals in the World Series, ultimately losing in a thrilling seven-game contest. In 1998, as part of Major League Baseball's realignment, the Brewers transitioned to the National League, joining the NL Central Division. This move marked a new chapter for the franchise, introducing them to a different set of rivals and an altered competitive landscape.
