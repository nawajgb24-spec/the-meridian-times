Airman Missing Since World War II Identified After 79 Years

Staff Sgt. Robert L. Kowal, an Army Air Forces airman declared missing in action during World War II, has finally been accounted for by the Defense POW/MIA Accounting Agency (DPAA). Kowal, a Milwaukee native, perished when his A-20G Havoc aircraft was shot down over Leyte, Philippines, on November 1, 1944. His remains were recovered in 2017 and identified through advanced scientific methods, bringing a nearly eight-decade search to a close for his family and the nation.

### Airman Missing Since World War II Identified After 79 Years **Washington, D.C.** — Seventy-nine years after his aircraft disappeared over the Philippines during World War II, Staff Sgt. Robert L. Kowal, a U.S. Army Air Forces airman, has been officially accounted for. The Defense POW/MIA Accounting Agency (DPAA) announced the identification of his remains, offering a profound sense of closure to a family and a nation that had long mourned his loss without certainty. Kowal, 20, of Milwaukee, Wisconsin, was the gunner aboard an A-20G Havoc aircraft when it was struck by enemy anti-aircraft fire on November 1, 1944. The announcement underscores the DPAA's ongoing commitment to recovering and identifying service members who made the ultimate sacrifice in past conflicts, regardless of the passage of time. ### Background Staff Sgt. Robert L. Kowal was assigned to the 386th Bombardment Squadron (Medium), 312th Bombardment Group (Light), 5th Air Force. His unit played a critical role in the Allied effort to liberate the Philippines from Japanese occupation during World War II, conducting vital bombing and strafing missions against enemy targets. On the fateful day of November 1, 1944, Kowal's A-20G Havoc (serial number 43-22129) took off from Tacloban Airfield on Leyte Island. The mission objective was a low-level attack on Japanese forces in the vicinity of San Pablo, Leyte, an area of intense combat as U.S. forces pressed their advance. During the attack, the aircraft, piloted by 1st Lt. James E. Lewis, was hit by heavy anti-aircraft fire. Witnesses reported seeing the plane crash and explode, leaving no survivors. Both Kowal and Lewis were declared Missing In Action (MIA) immediately following the incident. Post-war efforts to recover remains proved unsuccessful. In 1949, a military review board officially declared Staff Sgt. Kowal non-recoverable, his name later inscribed on the Walls of the Missing at the Manila American Cemetery and Memorial in the Philippines, a somber testament to the thousands who remain lost. ### Timeline **November 1, 1944:** Staff Sgt. Robert L. Kowal’s A-20G Havoc aircraft is shot down by anti-aircraft fire over Leyte Island, Philippines, during a combat mission. He and pilot 1st Lt. James E. Lewis are declared Missing In Action. **1949:** A U.S. military board reviews Kowal's case and declares his remains non-recoverable, concluding that no further recovery efforts could be made at the known crash site. **Late 20th/Early 21st Century:** The DPAA and its predecessors continue historical research into cases of unresolved missing service members from World War II, including those lost in the Pacific Theater. **2017:** A DPAA investigation team, working with local partners and historians, identifies a potential crash site in Leyte Province, Philippines. The team conducts an excavation and recovers human remains, along with associated aircraft wreckage and personal effects. **2017-2023:** The recovered remains are transferred to the DPAA laboratory at Offutt Air Force Base, Nebraska, for extensive forensic analysis, including anthropological examination, dental comparison, and mitochondrial DNA analysis, which relies on reference samples provided by Kowal’s surviving family members. **September 1, 2023:** The DPAA officially announces that Staff Sgt. Robert L. Kowal has been accounted for, confirming the identification of his remains after nearly eight decades. **June 1, 2024:** Staff Sgt. Robert L. Kowal is scheduled to be interred with full military honors at Arlington National Cemetery, Virginia. ### Latest Developments The identification of Staff Sgt. Kowal's remains marks a significant milestone in the DPAA's ongoing mission. The advanced forensic science capabilities, including comprehensive DNA analysis and anthropological study, were crucial in distinguishing Kowal's remains from other unidentified sets. This process often involves comparing bone fragments and dental records with historical data and family DNA samples to establish a definitive match. The DPAA operates global search and recovery teams, meticulously researching historical records, interviewing witnesses, and conducting arduous excavations in remote and often challenging environments. The success in Kowal's case highlights the agency's commitment to leveraging every available resource to fulfill its mandate. ### Analysis The accounting of Staff Sgt. Kowal underscores the enduring legacy of World War II and the nation's steadfast dedication to its fallen service members. For families, these identifications provide long-awaited answers and the opportunity for a proper burial, offering a tangible sense of closure that has been elusive for generations. Erosion, vegetation growth, and the scattering of remains pose significant obstacles. The successful identification in Kowal's case is a testament to persistent investigative techniques, technological advancements in forensics, and international collaboration. ### Public Response The news of Staff Sgt.
