Logan Square's identity is deeply rooted in its late 19th and early 20th-century development, characterized by elegant boulevards, majestic greystone buildings, and the iconic Illinois Centennial Monument. Conceived as part of Daniel Burnham’s larger plan for Chicago, the neighborhood’s tree-lined streets and public squares provided a verdant contrast to the dense urban core. Historically, it served as a gateway for immigrant communities, evolving through waves of German, Scandinavian, Polish, and Latin American residents, each contributing to its rich cultural tapestry. For decades, Logan Square maintained a somewhat understated profile, known primarily for its residential character and strong community bonds. However, starting in the early 2000s, the neighborhood began to experience a significant revitalization. Artists, chefs, and entrepreneurs were drawn to its affordable rents, spacious properties, and authentic atmosphere, gradually transforming its commercial corridors, particularly along Milwaukee Avenue.
