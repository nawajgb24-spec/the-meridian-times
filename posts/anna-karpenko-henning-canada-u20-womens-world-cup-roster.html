Anna Karpenko Henning Named to Canada's U-20 Women's World Cup Roster

Anna Karpenko Henning, a promising Canadian midfielder/defender, has secured a coveted spot on Canada's U-20 Women's National Team roster for the FIFA U-20 Women's World Cup Colombia 2024. The prestigious tournament is set to run from August 31 to September 22, featuring an expanded field of 24 nations. Henning's selection underscores her significant progress within Canada's youth development system and her strong collegiate performances. Canada finds itself in a challenging Group B, facing Brazil, France, and Fiji, as they prepare for intense competition on the international stage. This call-up marks a pivotal moment in Henning's career and highlights the ongoing development of women's football talent across Canada.

### Introduction Ottawa, ON – Anna Karpenko Henning, a dynamic midfielder and defender, has officially been named to Canada's U-20 Women's National Team roster for the FIFA U-20 Women's World Cup Colombia 2024. The highly anticipated tournament is scheduled to take place from August 31 to September 22 across four host cities in Colombia. Henning's selection marks a significant achievement, positioning her to represent Canada on one of the most prominent youth football stages globally. The announcement, made by Canada Soccer, confirms Henning's place among the 21 players tasked with competing against the world’s best youth talent. The inclusion underscores her consistent performance and potential as a key contributor to the national program. This edition of the FIFA U-20 Women's World Cup is notable for its expansion to 24 participating nations, reflecting the growing global interest and investment in women's football. ### Background Anna Karpenko Henning has steadily risen through the ranks of Canadian youth football. A versatile player capable of operating in both midfield and defensive roles, she has garnered recognition for her technical ability, tactical awareness, and commitment on the field. Her progression through various youth provincial and national team camps has been a testament to her dedication and development. Currently, Henning is a pivotal member of the University of Memphis women's soccer team, where she has been a consistent starter and contributor. Her collegiate career has provided a platform for continued growth, exposing her to high-level competition and rigorous training environments. This blend of domestic and collegiate experience has prepared her for the demands of international play. The FIFA U-20 Women's World Cup, established in 2002 (then as the FIFA U-19 Women's Championship), serves as a crucial proving ground for future senior national team stars. Canada has a history of strong participation, notably reaching the final in 2002 when hosting the inaugural event. The tournament is recognized for showcasing emerging talent and providing invaluable international experience for young players. ### Timeline Henning's football journey has progressed through several key stages. Her early career involved participation in high-level youth club programs within Canada, leading to identification by national team scouts. This pathway saw her attend various regional and national youth development camps, integrating her into Canada Soccer's talent identification system. Her commitment to the University of Memphis Tigers in the NCAA Division I collegiate system marked a significant step. Since joining, she has consistently been part of the squad, contributing to the team's successes and honing her skills in a competitive environment. These collegiate performances have further cemented her credentials for national team consideration. Canada's U-20 Women's National Team, under Head Coach Cindy Tye, has undergone an extensive preparation period leading up to the World Cup. This included a series of training camps, international friendlies, and tournaments designed to assess player readiness and build team cohesion. The final 21-player roster, including Henning, was officially confirmed and announced by Canada Soccer on June 27, 2024, culminating months of evaluation. ### Latest Developments Following the roster announcement, the Canadian U-20 squad is intensifying its preparations for the tournament. The team will undergo final training sessions and tactical reviews to ensure peak performance ahead of their departure for Colombia. Coach Cindy Tye has emphasized the importance of teamwork and tactical discipline as they face challenging opponents. Canada has been drawn into Group B for the group stage of the tournament. Their formidable group includes football powerhouses Brazil and France, alongside Fiji. This draw presents a significant test for the young Canadians, requiring them to perform at their highest level from the outset. The tournament format, with 24 teams, introduces an additional round of 16 following the group stage. The expanded format means that the top two teams from each of the six groups, along with the four best third-placed teams, will advance to the knockout rounds. This offers a slightly more forgiving pathway to the next stage compared to previous 16-team formats, yet the quality of opposition in Canada's group demands immediate readiness and consistent high-level play. ### Analysis Anna Karpenko Henning's inclusion is indicative of her balanced skill set, combining defensive solidity with an ability to contribute to build-up play from midfield.
