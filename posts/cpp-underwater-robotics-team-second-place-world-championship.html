Cal Poly Pomona Underwater Robotics Team Secures Second Place at Global Championship

The Cal Poly Pomona (CPP) Underwater Robotics Team, known as 'Hydrophytes,' achieved a remarkable second-place finish at the prestigious MATE International ROV Competition. This global championship, which took place in Long Beach, California, tested student teams from around the world on their ability to design, build, and operate advanced Remotely Operated Vehicles (ROVs) to address critical real-world challenges in marine environments. The team's accomplishment highlights the university's commitment to hands-on engineering education and solidifies its reputation as a hub for innovation in robotics and marine technology.

### Cal Poly Pomona Underwater Robotics Team Secures Second Place at Global Championship **LONG BEACH, CA** — The Cal Poly Pomona (CPP) Underwater Robotics Team, known as 'Hydrophytes,' demonstrated exceptional engineering prowess and competitive spirit by earning a distinguished second-place overall finish at the Marine Advanced Technology Education (MATE) International Remotely Operated Vehicle (ROV) Competition. This global championship, held in Long Beach, California, showcased the ingenuity of student teams from across the globe in tackling complex underwater challenges. The team's achievement marks a significant milestone for Cal Poly Pomona's College of Engineering and underscores the university's commitment to fostering practical, problem-solving skills among its students. The competition, which demands mastery in mechanical design, electrical systems, software programming, and effective communication, serves as a vital platform for cultivating the next generation of marine technologists and engineers. ### Background Cal Poly Pomona has a long-standing tradition of excellence in engineering education, emphasizing a learn-by-doing philosophy. Its engineering programs are consistently ranked among the best in the nation, attracting students passionate about applying theoretical knowledge to real-world applications. The university's involvement in competitive robotics is a direct extension of this pedagogical approach. The MATE International ROV Competition is a globally recognized event that challenges K-12 and university students to design and build ROVs to address real-world technical problems. These challenges often involve scenarios pertinent to ocean exploration, conservation, and industry. The competition provides a unique opportunity for students to apply classroom learning to practical engineering problems, work in interdisciplinary teams, and develop professional skills. Teams are judged not only on their ROV's performance in mission tasks but also on their technical reports, engineering presentations, and marketing displays. This comprehensive evaluation ensures that students develop a well-rounded set of skills crucial for future careers in STEM fields. ### Timeline **Early 2017:** The Cal Poly Pomona 'Hydrophytes' team embarked on the intensive design and fabrication phase for their ROV. The 2017 MATE competition theme, 'Innovations for a Sustainable Ocean,' presented specific requirements for tasks simulating operations in the North Sea's oil and gas industry and marine renewable energy sectors. **Spring 2017:** Over several months, the student engineers dedicated countless hours to refining their ROV prototype. This period involved rigorous testing of various subsystems, including propulsion, manipulation, sensing, and control. Iterative design improvements were crucial to optimizing the vehicle's capabilities for the anticipated mission tasks. **June 22-24, 2017:** The Cal Poly Pomona 'Hydrophytes' team traveled to Long Beach, California, to compete in the MATE International ROV Competition. Here, they joined top-tier teams from around the world, presenting their design, articulating their engineering process, and navigating their ROV through complex underwater scenarios at Long Beach City College. **June 24, 2017:** Following a demanding three days of competition, the Cal Poly Pomona 'Hydrophytes' team was officially awarded second place overall. This recognition underscored their outstanding performance across all judged categories, from technical innovation and robust design to effective teamwork and clear communication. ### Latest Developments While the specific achievement of the 'Hydrophytes' team occurred in 2017, its impact continues to resonate within Cal Poly Pomona's College of Engineering. The success served as a powerful testament to the caliber of the university's students and the effectiveness of its hands-on learning environment. The robotics program at Cal Poly Pomona has continued to thrive, attracting new talent and expanding its scope. Subsequent iterations of the CPP ROV team have continued to participate in regional and international competitions, building upon the knowledge base and best practices established by their predecessors. The university frequently highlights such past achievements in its outreach efforts, inspiring prospective students and showcasing the tangible outcomes of its engineering curriculum. This legacy of competitive success contributes to a vibrant culture of innovation and practical application within the university. ### Analysis The 2017 MATE competition's theme, 'Innovations for a Sustainable Ocean,' demanded a sophisticated blend of engineering solutions. Teams were tasked with operating their ROVs to simulate critical underwater operations such as inspecting deep-sea pipelines for leaks, maintaining subsea oil and gas infrastructure, and servicing offshore wind farms—all while navigating adverse conditions. The 'Hydrophytes' team distinguished itself through a robust and adaptable ROV design, coupled with a meticulously prepared technical report and a compelling engineering presentation. Their success was a result of an interdisciplinary effort, integrating mechanical, electrical, and computer engineering principles. The team's ability to diagnose and troubleshoot issues in real-time, often under pressure, further demonstrated their comprehensive understanding and practical problem-solving skills. Achieving second place globally against a field of highly competitive international teams signifies a high level of technical proficiency and collaborative execution. ### Public Response The announcement of Cal Poly Pomona's second-place finish was met with considerable pride and enthusiasm within the university community. Faculty, staff, and students celebrated the 'Hydrophytes' team's dedication and achievement, viewing it as a benchmark for engineering excellence.
