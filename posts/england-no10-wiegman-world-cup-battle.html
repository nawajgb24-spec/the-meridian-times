Wiegman's No. 10 Conundrum: A Catalyst for Success?

The competition for England's number 10 role under Sarina Wiegman, particularly leading up to and during the 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup, presented both challenges and opportunities. With established star Fran Kirby battling persistent injuries, the emergence of Ella Toone proved crucial. This article explores how Wiegman navigated these dynamics, leveraging squad depth and tactical flexibility to guide the Lionesses to the World Cup final, turning what could have been a significant problem into a clear positive for the national team.

The strategic battle for key positions within a national football squad often forms the bedrock of a team's potential success. For Sarina Wiegman, manager of the England women’s national team, the dynamic competition for the coveted number 10 role became a central talking point in the build-up to and during the 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup. This pivotal attacking midfield spot, traditionally occupied by a team’s primary playmaker, saw significant changes for the Lionesses, prompting debate over whether this was a challenge or a strategic advantage for Wiegman. The question loomed: would the fluidity and evolving nature of this position create instability, or would it showcase the depth and resilience of a squad aiming for global glory? The journey to the World Cup final revealed how Wiegman navigated these dynamics, transforming potential selection headaches into demonstrations of tactical adaptability and player development. **Background** The number 10 position is fundamental to many footballing systems, acting as the creative hub that links midfield and attack. In the context of the England women's team, Fran Kirby had long been the undisputed occupant of this role. Renowned for her vision, intricate passing, and ability to unlock defenses, Kirby was a lynchpin of the Lionesses' attack for many years, earning the moniker 'Mini Messi' for her deceptive agility and technical prowess. However, Kirby's career has been punctuated by persistent injury struggles, which frequently disrupted her rhythm and availability. As the 2023 World Cup approached, these fitness concerns intensified, casting uncertainty over her participation and, by extension, England's attacking blueprint. This created an opening for other talents to step forward, most notably Ella Toone, a player whose energetic style and knack for crucial goals offered a different, yet equally potent, dimension to the No. 10 role. **Timeline** Sarina Wiegman’s appointment as England manager in 2021 brought a new era of tactical discipline and high performance. Under her guidance, the team quickly developed, culminating in their historic victory at the UEFA Women's Euro 2022 on home soil. During this tournament, Fran Kirby largely retained her starting berth as the No. 10, contributing significantly with assists and linking play. Ella Toone, however, emerged as a vital impact player, often introduced from the bench to inject pace and creativity. Her memorable chip over the German goalkeeper in the Euro 2022 final at Wembley Stadium underscored her growing importance. As the focus shifted to the 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup in Australia and New Zealand, Kirby's fitness became a paramount concern. Throughout early 2023, she battled a knee injury that severely limited her club appearances for Chelsea. Despite her best efforts, the severity and timing of the injury ultimately led to her omission from Wiegman's provisional and final World Cup squads announced in May and June 2023, respectively. This decision, while necessary due to Kirby's health, solidified Ella Toone's status as the primary contender for the No. 10 role, or at least a significant attacking midfield presence. Wiegman's squad selection reflected a pragmatic approach, prioritising fit players ready for the rigours of a major tournament. Other players like Georgia Stanway, typically a deeper midfielder, also demonstrated the capability to push higher up the pitch, offering Wiegman further tactical options. Throughout the World Cup, Toone was a regular starter, scoring a pivotal goal in the semi-final against co-hosts Australia, further cementing her place in England's history. **Latest Developments** Following the 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup, where England reached the final, the dynamics of the number 10 position continue to evolve. Fran Kirby has steadily worked on her recovery, making appearances for Chelsea during the subsequent club season. Her return to full fitness will inevitably reignite discussions about her international future and potential re-integration into Sarina Wiegman's plans. Ella Toone, having excelled on the world stage, has maintained her prominent role within the Lionesses squad in subsequent international windows. Her consistent performances and crucial contributions have solidified her status as a key creative force for England. The emergence of other young talents who can operate in advanced midfield positions also adds to Wiegman's expanding pool of options. These developments indicate that while the specific "World Cup" pressure has passed, the healthy competition for attacking midfield roles remains a continuous aspect of England's squad building. **Analysis** The debate surrounding England's No. 10 role for the World Cup ultimately reveals more positives than problems for Sarina Wiegman. While the absence of a fully fit Fran Kirby, a player of immense quality, was undoubtedly a blow, it forced Wiegman to showcase the strength and adaptability inherent in her squad planning. This situation highlighted several key advantages. Firstly, it underscored the significant depth within the England setup. Ella Toone’s seamless transition into a starting role demonstrated that there was not only sufficient talent to cover for a major absence but also players capable of performing at the highest level under immense pressure. Her goals in both the Euro 2022 final and the 2023 World Cup semi-final are testament to her ability to deliver when it matters most, validating Wiegman's faith and the coaching staff's development work. Secondly, the competition, even if partly induced by injury, fostered a high-performance environment. Players knew that opportunities could arise, driving them to maintain peak fitness and form. This internal rivalry, managed effectively by Wiegman, ensures that complacency does not set in and that every player is motivated to earn their spot. Thirdly, it provided Wiegman with increased tactical flexibility. With various players capable of operating in or influencing the No. 10 space – whether it be Toone’s directness, Stanway’s box-to-box energy, or the potential for a returning Kirby’s finesse – England could adapt their attacking approach based on opponents or match situations. This adaptability is a hallmark of successful tournament teams. Rather than a problem, Wiegman expertly managed challenging circumstances to highlight the resilience and versatility of her squad, ultimately proving that depth and prepared alternatives are invaluable assets. **Public Response** The public and media response to the evolving No. 10 situation was largely understanding and appreciative of Sarina Wiegman's pragmatic approach. There was widespread sympathy for Fran Kirby, acknowledging her talent and the unfortunate timing of her injuries. Fans and pundits alike expressed disappointment that such a gifted player would miss a major tournament, but the sentiment quickly shifted to support for the players stepping up. Ella Toone’s performances, especially her crucial goals, were met with widespread acclaim. She became a fan favourite, embodying the Lionesses' fighting spirit and resilience. The narrative often praised Wiegman for her calm management style and her ability to build a squad capable of overcoming significant setbacks. The collective belief in the team's depth overshadowed any initial concerns about a potential "problem," transforming it into a story of collective strength and emergence. **Industry Impact** The management of the No. 10 role by Sarina Wiegman, particularly the successful integration of Ella Toone in Kirby's absence, carries significant implications for women's football as an industry. It highlights the increasing professionalisation and depth of talent within national teams. The ability of a squad to maintain elite performance despite the absence of a star player due to injury underscores the effectiveness of talent identification, development pathways, and comprehensive sports science support. This situation also reinforces the value of tactical flexibility and robust squad management at the highest levels of the sport. Coaches are increasingly expected to cultivate multiple options for key positions, moving beyond reliance on a single playmaker. For club football, it elevates the profiles of players like Toone, demonstrating their market value and appeal. More broadly, England's sustained success, partly enabled by this squad depth, contributes to the growing commercial viability and global appeal of women's football, encouraging further investment and growth across the ecosystem. **Future Outlook** Looking ahead, the competition for England’s No. 10 role is likely to remain a healthy and dynamic aspect of Sarina Wiegman’s squad selections. Fran Kirby’s potential return to full fitness would present Wiegman with a genuine selection dilemma, but one born of strength rather than necessity. A fully fit Kirby alongside a proven Ella Toone would provide an enviable array of creative options, allowing for varied tactical approaches and effective rotation. Beyond these two, the continuous development of young attacking midfielders within the English football system suggests that Wiegman will continue to have a rich pool of talent to draw from. The long-term outlook points to Wiegman fostering an environment where competition elevates individual performances, ensuring England remains a formidable force in international women's football for tournaments like the UEFA Women's Euro 2025 and the next FIFA Women's World Cup. The success at the 2023 World Cup, despite key absences, demonstrated a resilient foundation for future achievements. **Conclusion** The question of whether the fight to be England’s No. 10 at the World Cup was a problem or a positive for Sarina Wiegman has been answered with clarity. While Fran Kirby’s injury-enforced absence presented a significant challenge, Wiegman’s astute management, coupled with the squad’s inherent depth and Ella Toone’s stellar emergence, transformed a potential hindrance into a definitive strength. The Lionesses reached the World Cup final, showcasing resilience and tactical versatility that were direct outcomes of their ability to adapt. This scenario underscored the importance of comprehensive squad planning and player development in modern international football. Far from being a problem, the evolving landscape of England's No. 10 role highlighted Wiegman’s capacity to navigate adversity, fostering a competitive yet cohesive environment that propelled her team to the pinnacle of the sport. It stands as a testament to the growth of English women's football and its readiness to face future challenges with a deep and talented pool of players.
