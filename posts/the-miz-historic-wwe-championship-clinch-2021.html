The Miz's Decadal Journey Culminates in Historic WWE Championship Win

Mike 'The Miz' Mizanin's second WWE Championship victory in February 2021 marked a significant career milestone, arriving a decade after his initial reign. His successful Money in the Bank cash-in on Drew McIntyre at Elimination Chamber, following a period of anticipation and a previous nullified attempt, fulfilled a 'long time coming' narrative. This article details the context of his win, its immediate aftermath, and its lasting impact on The Miz's legacy and the professional wrestling landscape.

### Introduction In the unpredictable landscape of professional wrestling, rare moments encapsulate a performer's enduring journey. For Mike Mizanin, globally recognized as The Miz, February 21, 2021, delivered such a moment. After a prolonged period of holding the coveted Money in the Bank contract—a period that could metaphorically be described as a 'scoreless start' in terms of title success—The Miz seized his opportunity, orchestrating a 'historic clinch' for the WWE Championship. His triumph at Elimination Chamber, cashing in on a vulnerable Drew McIntyre, was not merely another title change. It represented the culmination of a decade-long wait for The Miz to reclaim professional wrestling's most prestigious prize, cementing a narrative of perseverance and strategic opportunism that resonated with fans and critics alike. The phrase 'long time coming' became a fitting descriptor for a superstar who had consistently remained relevant at the highest level. ### Background The Miz carved out a unique career trajectory in WWE, initially gaining fame through reality television before transitioning into professional wrestling. Debuting in 2004, he evolved from a polarizing rookie into a charismatic, arrogant 'A-Lister' whose mic skills became as renowned as his in-ring prowess. His first WWE Championship reign in 2010, lasting 160 days, saw him headline WrestleMania XXVII against John Cena, a definitive moment that established him as a main event player. Despite holding numerous other championships—including eight Intercontinental Championships, two United States Championships, and multiple Tag Team Championships—the WWE Championship eluded him for ten years. This decade saw him consistently featured on television, often in prominent storylines, but rarely at the absolute pinnacle of the singles division. This sustained presence without the top gold contributed to the perception of a 'long time coming' for another main event run. The Money in the Bank contract, won at Hell in a Cell in October 2020, provided The Miz with a guaranteed championship opportunity. The briefcase bestows upon its holder the right to cash in on any champion, at any time, within a year. This mechanism frequently provides some of the most dramatic and unexpected championship changes in WWE history, relying on opportune timing and often villainous tactics. ### Timeline **October 25, 2020:** The Miz, with assistance from John Morrison, wins the Money in the Bank contract at the Hell in a Cell pay-per-view. This victory immediately positioned him as a looming threat to both the WWE and Universal Champions. **December 20, 2020:** An initial attempt to cash in the contract on then-WWE Champion Drew McIntyre at TLC: Tables, Ladders & Chairs proves unsuccessful. The match was later ruled null and void due to a technicality, with The Miz regaining possession of the briefcase, prolonging the anticipation of his next move. **February 21, 2021:** The Elimination Chamber pay-per-view commences with Drew McIntyre defending his WWE Championship inside the Elimination Chamber against five other competitors. McIntyre successfully retains his title after a grueling encounter. **February 21, 2021 (Immediately after Elimination Chamber match):** Following McIntyre's strenuous defense, Bobby Lashley unexpectedly attacks the fatigued champion, brutalizing him both inside and outside the ring. This opportune moment created the perfect scenario for The Miz. **February 21, 2021:** Seizing the moment of a severely weakened McIntyre, The Miz rushes to the ring, hands his Money in the Bank briefcase to the referee, and officially cashes in. A Skull-Crushing Finale to the incapacitated champion secures The Miz his second WWE Championship, igniting widespread reaction. **March 1, 2021:** The Miz's second reign as WWE Champion proves to be short-lived. Just eight days after his triumph, he loses the title to Bobby Lashley on Monday Night Raw, a direct consequence of the alliance formed between Miz and Lashley's manager, MVP, prior to the cash-in. ### Latest Developments The immediate aftermath of The Miz's championship win was characterized by intense debate and storyline progression. His brief, eight-day reign as WWE Champion became a pivotal plot point, illustrating the cyclical nature of alliances and betrayals within professional wrestling. The win served primarily as a catalyst for Bobby Lashley's ascent to the WWE Championship, marking a significant moment for Lashley as well. The Miz's short time as champion saw him attempt to use every strategic advantage available, including the contractual clause allowing him to defend the title eight days later on Raw, instead of the following week. This desperation underscored his character's fear of losing the title he had worked so long to regain. His eventual loss to Lashley was seen as a logical progression, given the circumstances of his initial win and the escalating tension with Lashley. ### Analysis The strategic booking of The Miz's second WWE Championship victory demonstrated WWE's ability to craft compelling narratives around established veterans. The 'scoreless start' prior to the 'historic clinch' effectively highlighted The Miz's decade-long pursuit of the top title. His character's core traits—cunning, opportunism, and self-belief—were amplified by the Money in the Bank cash-in, a tactic perfectly suited to his villainous persona. The decision to have him win the title, even for a short period, reinforced his credibility as a main event talent while simultaneously advancing other storylines. It validated his consistency and dedication to the company, rewarding a performer who had been a reliable fixture for well over a decade. The 'long time coming' sentiment resonated because The Miz had consistently performed at a high level without being the focal point of the main event scene for an extended period. ### Public Response The public response to The Miz's championship win was mixed, reflecting the diverse opinions within the wrestling fan base. Many long-time followers expressed satisfaction, acknowledging his longevity and hard work. Social media platforms buzzed with discussions about whether he deserved the reign, irrespective of its duration, given his consistent performance over the years. His detractors, conversely, criticized the short nature of the reign, viewing it as a transitional moment rather than a true main event push for The Miz. Wrestling journalists and pundits generally praised the narrative execution, particularly the use of Bobby Lashley to facilitate the cash-in, which added layers to multiple ongoing feuds. While some lamented that The Miz did not receive a longer, more substantial championship run, the overall consensus was that the moment provided a memorable and dramatic conclusion to his Money in the Bank arc and served its purpose within the broader WWE narrative. ### Industry Impact The Miz's championship win had a noticeable impact on the industry, particularly in reinforcing the power and prestige of the Money in the Bank contract. The successful cash-in, following a period where the briefcase's effectiveness had sometimes been questioned, re-established it as a legitimate threat to any champion. It showcased how a strategic cash-in could quickly alter the landscape of the main event scene. Furthermore, the event significantly elevated Bobby Lashley, who transitioned from attacking McIntyre to decisively defeating The Miz for the championship. This series of events positioned Lashley as a dominant, credible champion, embarking on his own formidable reign. The angle also provided Drew McIntyre with a sympathetic challenger narrative, setting up future confrontations with both Miz and Lashley. The ripple effect touched multiple top-tier superstars and storylines. ### Future Outlook Following his brief second WWE Championship reign, The Miz continued to be a prominent figure on WWE programming. While he did not immediately re-enter the main event championship picture, his status as a two-time WWE Champion solidified his legacy as a genuine main eventer. This win ensured he would forever be listed among the elite few who have held the company's most coveted title multiple times. His role shifted towards that of a respected, though still arrogant, veteran. He continued to engage in high-profile feuds, participate in significant pay-per-view matches, and maintain his status as a reliable on-screen personality. The second WWE Championship marked a capstone moment in a career already replete with accolades, affirming his place as one of WWE's most enduring and versatile performers, capable of contributing to any storyline across various positions on the card. ### Conclusion The Miz's second WWE Championship victory in February 2021 was a strategically executed moment that transcended a simple title change. It was a testament to his longevity, character work, and unwavering commitment over more than 15 years in professional wrestling. The 'scoreless start' of waiting a decade and holding the Money in the Bank briefcase ultimately led to a 'historic clinch' that enriched his storied career. This triumph, while brief, served as a crucial narrative device, propelling other superstars to prominence while simultaneously validating The Miz's position as a multi-time main event champion. It reaffirmed the dramatic potential of WWE's storytelling and provided a memorable chapter in the career of one of its most recognizable and polarizing figures, underscoring that for some, great success is truly 'long time coming'.
