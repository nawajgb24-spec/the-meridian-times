Commanders Showcase Dual Threat in Preseason Victory Over Bengals

The Washington Commanders concluded their 2023 NFL preseason with a 21-19 victory over the Cincinnati Bengals, a game defined by both explosive offensive plays and a resolute defensive performance. Quarterback Sam Howell demonstrated command of the offense, orchestrating key scoring drives, while the defense delivered critical turnovers and stops. This performance provided a significant boost in confidence and clarity regarding roster decisions ahead of the regular season, particularly solidifying Howell's position as the starting quarterback.

Washington, D.C. — The Washington Commanders concluded their 2023 NFL preseason campaign with a hard-fought 21-19 victory against the Cincinnati Bengals at FedExField on August 26, 2023. The contest served as a final showcase for players vying for roster spots and offered a glimpse into the team's potential ahead of the regular season. The game highlighted a significant dual-threat performance from the Commanders, characterized by timely offensive explosiveness and a defense that consistently delivered in crucial moments. This win marked a perfect 3-0 preseason record for Washington, building momentum as the team transitioned towards competitive play. ### Background Entering the final preseason matchup, both the Commanders and Bengals approached the game with distinct objectives. For Washington, the primary goal was to evaluate quarterback Sam Howell's readiness to lead the offense and finalize key position battles across the roster. The team sought to build cohesion and confidence under new offensive coordinator Eric Bieniemy. The Cincinnati Bengals, conversely, were largely focused on assessing their depth, particularly at quarterback, with starter Joe Burrow sidelined due to a calf injury. Their preseason strategy involved giving significant snaps to backup quarterbacks Jake Browning and Trevor Siemian, alongside evaluating bubble players. ### Timeline The game commenced with both teams exchanging early drives, testing their offensive and defensive schemes. Commanders quarterback Sam Howell quickly established a rhythm, connecting with receivers and extending plays to move the chains. His command of the first-team offense was evident from the opening series. Washington's offense generated the first major scoring play when running back Chris Rodriguez Jr. powered into the end zone for a touchdown. This marked a continuation of his strong preseason showing, demonstrating his potential impact in the ground game. Later in the first half, Howell capped another effective drive with a touchdown pass to wide receiver Mitchell Tinsley. This play underscored the developing chemistry within Washington's receiving corps and Howell's ability to deliver under pressure. The Commanders' defense asserted its presence throughout the game. Rookie defensive backs Jartavius Martin and Quan Martin each secured an interception, halting Bengals drives and showcasing opportunistic playmaking. The defense also pressured Bengals quarterbacks, registering three sacks, including contributions from Shaka Toney and KJ Henry. The Bengals mounted a late-game surge, with Chris Evans scoring a rushing touchdown and Trevor Siemian connecting for a passing touchdown. Despite Cincinnati's efforts to close the gap, Washington's defense held firm in the final moments, preserving their narrow two-point lead for the 21-19 victory. ### Latest Developments In the immediate aftermath of the game, the primary narrative surrounding the Commanders centered on Sam Howell's performance. His efficient passing, decision-making, and ability to navigate pressure solidified his position as the undisputed starting quarterback for the regular season, a decision confirmed by the coaching staff shortly after. Defensive coordinator Jack Del Rio lauded the unit's ability to create turnovers and maintain composure in critical situations. The strong performances from young defensive backs, in particular, provided encouraging signs for the team's depth in the secondary. Post-game discussions highlighted individual player battles that were decisively won or lost, leading to difficult roster decisions. ### Analysis The Commanders' victory against the Bengals offered valuable insights into the team's strengths and areas for development. On offense, Howell's poised performance suggested a potential for stability at the quarterback position, a crucial factor for Washington's aspirations. The combination of effective rushing from Chris Rodriguez Jr. and reliable receiving targets provided a balanced offensive attack. Defensively, the Commanders showcased a unit capable of disrupting opposing offenses through pressure and generating turnovers. The two interceptions were not merely statistical events but represented critical momentum shifts that prevented the Bengals from gaining control. This defensive 'business-like' approach demonstrated resilience and discipline. For the Bengals, the game underscored the importance of depth, especially with Joe Burrow's injury. While Jake Browning and Trevor Siemian had moments, the offensive rhythm was inconsistent. The preseason provided valuable tape for their coaching staff to evaluate contingency plans and refine schemes. ### Public Response The public response to the Commanders' preseason finale was largely positive, particularly among the fan base. Social media platforms and local sports radio programs buzzed with optimism regarding Sam Howell's development and the defense's consistent playmaking. Many viewed the perfect preseason record as a positive indicator of the team's potential. Sports analysts, while acknowledging the inherent limitations of preseason results, noted Washington's improved offensive efficiency and defensive aggression. Commentary often highlighted the collective effort, emphasizing that the team appeared to be gelling under the new coaching structure. The performance sparked renewed hope for a more competitive regular season. ### Industry Impact The performance against the Bengals, and indeed the entire 3-0 preseason, had a measurable impact within the broader NFL landscape. For the Commanders, it shifted perceptions regarding their competitiveness, moving them from a team with significant question marks to one with emergent promise. Sam Howell's stock, in particular, rose significantly within league circles. The demonstrated defensive prowess and offensive flashes also influenced early season projections and betting lines, albeit modestly for a preseason outcome. Scouting departments across the league took note of individual player performances, impacting the waiver wire and practice squad decisions as teams finalized their rosters. For players like Chris Rodriguez Jr. and the rookie defensive backs, this game helped solidify their positions. ### Future Outlook Looking ahead, the Commanders entered the regular season with a clear starting quarterback and a defensive unit that had demonstrated its capability to create game-changing plays. The challenge for Washington was to translate this preseason momentum into sustained success in competitive regular-season matchups against higher-caliber opponents. For the Bengals, the focus remained on Joe Burrow's recovery and integrating him back into the offense. The preseason offered valuable reps for their backup quarterbacks and an opportunity to identify areas where depth might be tested. Both teams used the insights from this game to fine-tune strategies and personnel decisions for the demanding NFL schedule. ### Conclusion The Washington Commanders' 21-19 victory over the Cincinnati Bengals in the final preseason game was more than just another exhibition win. It represented a significant confidence boost, showcasing a team capable of both 'explosive playmaking' on offense and a 'stands on business' mentality on defense. Sam Howell's emergence and the defense's consistent performance provided a foundation of optimism for the season ahead. As the regular season commenced, the Commanders aimed to leverage this dual threat, hoping the blend of offensive fireworks and defensive fortitude would propel them to a more successful campaign. The game served as a clear statement of intent for a team looking to establish a new identity in the league.
