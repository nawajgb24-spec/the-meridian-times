The anticipation surrounding any new Precure film is substantial, and any hint of fresh character additions or a new cinematic direction is often met with keen interest from its dedicated global fanbase. ## Background The Precure franchise, known in Japan as Pretty Cure, launched in 2004 with 'Futari wa Precure' and quickly established itself as a cornerstone of the magical girl genre. Created by Izumi Todo and produced by Toei Animation, the series follows a new group of magical girls each year, focusing on themes of friendship, courage, justice, and self-belief. This annual cycle of new television series has been consistently complemented by theatrical film releases, often tying into the current TV season's storyline or featuring an 'All Stars' crossover event. These films play a crucial role in expanding the Precure universe, offering cinematic-scale adventures and often introducing unique characters that exist solely within the movie's narrative. The inclusion of original characters allows for fresh plotlines and emotional arcs that resonate with both new viewers and long-time fans, without directly impacting the ongoing television series continuity. Over nearly two decades, the franchise has cultivated an expansive roster of heroines, villains, and supporting characters, cementing its status as one of Japan's most recognizable and successful animation properties. The theatrical outings are a testament to its enduring popularity and commercial viability, frequently topping box office charts for children's films. ## Timeline The typical development and promotional cycle for a Precure film usually spans several months. After an initial announcement or teaser, often accompanying the conclusion of a previous film or the launch of a new TV series, further details progressively emerge. Precure films generally see two main release windows annually: a spring release, often associated with the 'Precure All Stars' series, and an autumn release, typically a standalone film for the current TV series.
