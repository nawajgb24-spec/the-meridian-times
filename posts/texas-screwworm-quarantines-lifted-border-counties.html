Texas Lifts Screwworm Quarantines in Border Counties

Quarantine restrictions in Cameron and Hidalgo Counties, Texas, imposed due to New World screwworm detections, have been lifted by the USDA. This announcement, made by Congressman Vicente Gonzalez, signifies a successful eradication effort for the parasitic pest that threatened livestock and wildlife. The multi-agency response utilized the Sterile Insect Technique to contain and eliminate the screwworm, which had been absent from the continental U.S. for decades, bringing relief to the agricultural sector.

### Texas Border Counties See Screwworm Quarantines Lifted **AUSTIN, Texas** — Federal authorities have lifted two New World screwworm (NWS) quarantine areas in Texas, marking a significant victory in the ongoing battle against the destructive livestock parasite. The announcement, confirmed by U.S. Congressman Vicente Gonzalez, signals the successful eradication of the pest from Cameron and Hidalgo Counties, regions that had faced strict animal movement restrictions since October 2023. The USDA’s Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) implemented the quarantines following detections of New World screwworm in domestic animals. The swift federal and local response aimed to prevent wider infestation, which could have devastating consequences for Texas's vast agricultural industry and wildlife populations. ### Background of a Persistent Threat New World screwworms (scientific name *Cochliomyia hominivorax*) are parasitic larvae that infest open wounds of warm-blooded animals, including livestock, wildlife, and occasionally humans. These insidious parasites burrow into flesh, feeding on living tissue and causing severe pain, secondary infections, and ultimately, if untreated, death. Historically, screwworms were a major agricultural scourge across the American South, posing an immense economic burden on ranchers and farmers. Their eradication from the continental United States in 1966 was hailed as one of the greatest achievements in veterinary science, largely due to the innovative Sterile Insect Technique (SIT). The SIT involves mass-rearing screwworm flies, sterilizing the male flies with radiation, and then releasing millions of these sterile males into infested areas. When sterile males mate with wild females, the eggs produced are infertile, leading to a rapid decline in the wild population over several generations. This highly effective method has been crucial in maintaining the U.S. free of sustained NWS populations for decades. Despite the continental U.S. being officially screwworm-free since 1966, the threat of reintroduction remains constant, particularly in border regions with Mexico and other tropical areas where the pest persists. Occasional incursions or isolated outbreaks have occurred, necessitating immediate and aggressive eradication protocols to prevent re-establishment. ### Timeline of the Recent Outbreak and Eradication **October 2023:** Federal authorities, specifically USDA APHIS, confirmed the presence of New World screwworms in animals within Cameron and Hidalgo Counties in South Texas. The initial detections prompted immediate concern due to the proximity to the U.S.-Mexico border and the high density of livestock and wildlife in the region. Following the confirmation, APHIS swiftly imposed quarantines on both counties. These quarantines restricted the movement of susceptible animals, including cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, horses, and certain wildlife, out of the affected areas without inspection and official certification. Throughout late 2023 and early 2024, an intensive eradication campaign was launched. This involved on-the-ground surveillance, inspection of domestic animals, and the widespread release of sterile male screwworm flies. Millions of sterile flies, primarily sourced from the USDA's screwworm production facility in Panama, were deployed over the designated areas to disrupt the reproductive cycle of any wild screwworm populations. Animal health officials worked closely with local ranchers, veterinarians, and state agencies to educate the public on identifying screwworm infestations and reporting suspected cases. Compliance with quarantine protocols was critical to containing the spread and facilitating eradication efforts. ### Latest Developments: Quarantines Lifted The formal announcement in January 2024 by U.S. Congressman Vicente Gonzalez confirmed that the USDA APHIS had officially lifted the quarantines in both Cameron and Hidalgo Counties. This decision was made after a rigorous period of surveillance confirmed no new screwworm detections within the required timeframe, indicating that the eradication efforts were successful. The lifting of these restrictions means that producers in Cameron and Hidalgo Counties can now move their animals freely without the burden of additional inspections or permits related to the screwworm threat. This development comes as a significant relief to the local agricultural community, which relies heavily on the unimpeded movement of livestock for market and pasture. ### Analysis: A Testament to Vigilance and Collaboration The swift containment and eradication of the New World screwworm in these Texas counties underscore the effectiveness of established emergency response protocols and the Sterile Insect Technique. The rapid deployment of resources and the collaborative efforts between federal agencies like APHIS, state departments of agriculture, and local stakeholders proved instrumental. This incident serves as a critical reminder of the constant vigilance required to protect the U.S. livestock industry from foreign animal diseases and pests. The successful outcome highlights the importance of sustained funding and scientific expertise dedicated to animal health security, particularly along vulnerable border regions. While the immediate threat has been neutralized, the potential for future incursions remains. Continuous monitoring, robust biosecurity measures, and international cooperation with countries where NWS is endemic are essential to maintaining the screwworm-free status of the United States. ### Public Response: Relief and Renewed Confidence The lifting of the quarantines has been met with widespread relief by ranchers, livestock producers, and agricultural associations in the affected Texas counties. The restrictions had posed logistical and financial challenges, disrupting routine operations and causing uncertainty within the industry. Local communities and pet owners also expressed gratitude for the rapid response, as screwworms can also affect pets and, in rare cases, humans. The transparency of government agencies in communicating the threat and the progress of eradication efforts helped to foster public trust and cooperation during the quarantine period. Agricultural organizations have praised the coordinated efforts, viewing the successful eradication as a testament to the resilience of the local farming community and the efficacy of federal animal health programs. ### Industry Impact: Resuming Normal Operations The direct impact of the quarantines was primarily felt by the livestock industry in Cameron and Hidalgo Counties. Ranchers faced increased costs associated with veterinary inspections, restricted movement of animals for grazing or sale, and the potential for direct losses if animals became infested. With the quarantines lifted, the immediate economic pressure on these producers is alleviated. Livestock markets can resume normal operations, and the free movement of animals will support ranching efficiency and profitability. The broader impact on the regional economy, which relies significantly on agriculture, is also positive, as confidence in animal health security is restored. Furthermore, the prevention of a widespread outbreak averted potentially catastrophic losses for the state's multi-billion dollar cattle industry and protected native wildlife populations, which are also susceptible to screwworm infestations. ### Future Outlook: Maintaining the Barrier The lifting of the quarantines does not signal the end of vigilance. Border regions like South Texas remain on the front lines against potential reintroductions of pests and diseases. USDA APHIS and its partners will continue enhanced surveillance in the region to quickly detect any new threats. Educational programs for ranchers and the public on early detection and reporting will persist. Investment in biosecurity measures on farms and ranches, coupled with ongoing research into pest control technologies, will be crucial. The maintenance of the sterile screwworm fly facility in Panama is vital as a continuous buffer and rapid response resource. International cooperation with Mexico and other neighboring countries also plays a critical role in controlling screwworm populations at their source, thereby reducing the risk of cross-border incursions into the United States. ### Conclusion: A Victory for Animal Health The successful eradication of New World screwworm from Cameron and Hidalgo Counties and the subsequent lifting of quarantines represent a significant achievement in animal health security. This outcome demonstrates the effectiveness of targeted intervention, scientific methods like SIT, and strong collaboration between governmental agencies and the agricultural community. While the immediate threat has been neutralized, the incident serves as a powerful reminder that the fight against invasive pests is continuous. Sustained vigilance, robust surveillance, and a commitment to proactive measures will be essential to ensure that the continental United States remains free from this destructive parasite, safeguarding its livestock, wildlife, and agricultural economy for the future.
