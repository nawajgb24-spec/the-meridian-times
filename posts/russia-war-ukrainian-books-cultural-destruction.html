Ukraine's Literary Front: Russia's War on Books and Culture

Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine has extended beyond military objectives to include a systematic assault on Ukrainian cultural heritage. The destruction of book warehouses, libraries, and publishing infrastructure, coupled with the confiscation and burning of Ukrainian-language texts in occupied territories, suggests a deliberate strategy to erase Ukrainian national identity. This article details the extent of this cultural warfare, its impact on the Ukrainian publishing industry and society, and the ongoing efforts to preserve the nation's literary soul amidst conflict.

### Introduction Since the full-scale invasion began in February 2022, Russia's war in Ukraine has manifested on multiple fronts. Beyond the immediate military conflict, there is a discernible campaign targeting Ukrainian cultural identity, with books and literature emerging as specific points of contention. This effort encompasses the physical destruction of cultural infrastructure and the ideological suppression of Ukrainian-language materials. Reports from across Ukraine detail instances where Russian forces have incinerated novels, destroyed publishing warehouses, and systematically removed Ukrainian texts from libraries and educational institutions. These actions are seen by many as part of a broader strategy to undermine Ukrainian nationhood and replace it with a Russian narrative, extending the conflict into the realm of cultural heritage. ### Background The suppression of Ukrainian language and culture by Russia is not a new phenomenon. Historical precedents include tsarist decrees in the 19th century prohibiting the use of the Ukrainian language in print, and Soviet-era policies of Russification. This long history provides context for current events, suggesting that the contemporary destruction of Ukrainian books is not merely collateral damage but rather a continuation of historical patterns aimed at cultural subjugation. Ukraine's independent cultural sphere has flourished since 1991, with a vibrant publishing industry supporting a growing body of Ukrainian-language literature and scholarship. This cultural resurgence, closely tied to national identity, appears to be a specific target in the ongoing conflict. ### Timeline The full-scale invasion in February 2022 marked the beginning of widespread damage to cultural sites. Initial months saw reports of libraries and cultural centers being hit in cities such as Mariupol, Chernihiv, and Kharkiv due to shelling and missile strikes. Many of these facilities housed extensive collections of Ukrainian literature. By the summer of 2022, as Russian forces occupied various territories, evidence began to surface of deliberate actions against Ukrainian books. In occupied towns and villages, reports indicated that Russian soldiers were confiscating Ukrainian-language books from school libraries and public institutions. These materials were often removed, discarded, or reportedly burned. Throughout late 2022 and 2023, the systematic replacement of Ukrainian curricula and textbooks with Russian versions became a documented policy in occupied areas. This included importing Russian-published literature and educational materials, while Ukrainian equivalents were actively purged. This process extended to public libraries, where Ukrainian books were removed from shelves, often replaced by Russian propaganda. One of the most significant recent incidents occurred in May 2024, when a Russian missile strike directly hit a major warehouse belonging to Vivat, one of Ukraine's largest publishing houses, in Kharkiv. The strike resulted in a large fire that incinerated hundreds of thousands of books, representing a substantial loss of inventory and cultural output. ### Latest Developments The destruction of the Vivat warehouse underscored the ongoing vulnerability of Ukraine's cultural infrastructure. Kateryna Pereverzeva, CEO of Vivat, stated that the losses were immense, affecting both current stock and the ability to distribute new releases. This incident prompted renewed calls for international support for Ukraine's cultural sector. In currently occupied regions, reports continue to indicate efforts by Russian authorities to enforce a Russian cultural and educational agenda. This includes further removal of Ukrainian texts from institutions and the promotion of Russian-language media and literature as the sole acceptable cultural products. Access to Ukrainian media and literature remains severely restricted in these areas. ### Analysis The targeting of Ukrainian books and cultural institutions is widely interpreted as a deliberate strategy of cultural destruction, sometimes referred to as 'culturicide.' Experts and Ukrainian officials argue that these actions are not merely accidental consequences of war but a calculated attempt to erase Ukrainian identity, language, and historical narrative. This strategy aligns with the broader goals of 'Russification' and 'de-Ukrainization' in territories under Russian control. By eliminating Ukrainian books and replacing them with Russian alternatives, Moscow appears to aim at severing the cultural ties that bind Ukrainians to their national heritage, thereby facilitating political and social integration into the Russian sphere. The destruction of cultural memory is seen as a precursor to political subjugation. ### Public Response The Ukrainian government and cultural organizations have strongly condemned these actions, highlighting them as war crimes and violations of international humanitarian law. The Ministry of Culture and Information Policy of Ukraine has been actively documenting damages and losses to cultural heritage, including libraries and archives. Ukrainian authors, publishers, and librarians have voiced their outrage and defiance, emphasizing the resilience of Ukrainian culture. Efforts are underway to preserve remaining collections, digitize vulnerable archives, and continue publishing new Ukrainian works despite the immense challenges. Internationally, organizations like UNESCO and PEN International have expressed concern and provided support for cultural preservation initiatives in Ukraine. ### Industry Impact The Ukrainian publishing industry has been profoundly affected. The destruction of printing houses, warehouses, and bookstores has led to significant financial losses, disruption of supply chains, and a drastic reduction in production and distribution capacity. Many publishers have lost their entire back catalogues and current inventories, jeopardizing their ability to recover. Authors face challenges in getting their work published and distributed, while readers in many areas have lost access to Ukrainian literature. Despite these hardships, there has been a notable surge in demand for Ukrainian-language books in free Ukraine, reflecting a strengthening of national identity and a desire to connect with cultural heritage amidst the conflict. Publishers are adapting by exploring digital distribution and seeking international partnerships. ### Future Outlook The long-term implications of Russia's cultural offensive are substantial. Rebuilding damaged infrastructure, replenishing lost collections, and restoring a vibrant literary ecosystem will require considerable time and resources. The ideological battle over Ukrainian identity, however, is likely to persist even after military hostilities cease. Ukrainian cultural institutions, with international assistance, are committed to reversing the damage. This includes projects focused on digitizing rare books, cataloging destroyed collections, and promoting Ukrainian literature globally. The resilience of Ukrainian writers and publishers, alongside strong public demand for national literature, indicates a determined effort to safeguard cultural autonomy. ### Conclusion The systematic targeting of Ukrainian books and cultural institutions by Russian forces represents a critical dimension of the ongoing war, aimed at severing the roots of Ukrainian national identity. From incinerated novels in warehouses to purged library shelves in occupied cities, the conflict is as much a battle for cultural survival as it is for territorial integrity. While the physical losses are immense, the spirit of Ukrainian literature and the resolve to preserve it demonstrate a resistance that extends beyond the battlefield, asserting the enduring power of words in the face of destruction.
