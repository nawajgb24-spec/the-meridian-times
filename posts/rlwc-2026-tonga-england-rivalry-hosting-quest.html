England, a founding nation of the sport, has consistently been a formidable contender, reaching multiple finals and semi-finals. Tonga's journey to rugby league prominence is a more recent and compelling narrative. Historically a tier-two nation, Tonga exploded onto the international scene in the 2017 World Cup, reaching the semi-finals in a remarkable underdog run. Their thrilling 20-18 semi-final loss to England, marked by a controversial refereeing decision in the final moments, cemented their status as a global force and created one of the sport's most memorable rivalries. This rivalry is characterised by contrasting styles: England's structured play and robust defence versus Tonga's expansive, aggressive, and often unpredictable attacking flair. Both nations command immense fan loyalty, with significant diaspora communities contributing to passionate support both in stadiums and online. The 2017 clash, in particular, transcended the sport, becoming a cultural touchstone for Tongan communities worldwide. ### Timeline **1954:** The inaugural Rugby League World Cup is held in France, marking the beginning of international rugby league's marquee event. **2017:** The Rugby League World Cup in Australia, New Zealand, and Papua New Guinea sees Tonga's meteoric rise.
