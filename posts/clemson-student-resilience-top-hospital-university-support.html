Clemson Philosophy Major Navigates Health Crisis with Support of Premier Hospital and University

The remarkable journey of Anna Leigh Stang, a Clemson University philosophy major, illustrates the convergence of world-class medical care and a supportive academic environment. Diagnosed with rare and complex conditions, Stang sought treatment at the Mayo Clinic while continuing her studies at Clemson, a university renowned for its student experience. Her story highlights the profound impact of comprehensive support systems on individuals facing significant health challenges during their educational pursuits.

### Introduction In an intersection of advanced medical care and dedicated academic support, the journey of Clemson University philosophy major Anna Leigh Stang stands as a notable example of resilience. Stang, confronted with a rare and debilitating health condition, found herself navigating the complexities of treatment at the Mayo Clinic, an institution frequently cited as a global leader in medical care. Simultaneously, she has maintained her academic progress at Clemson University, known for its commitment to student experience. Her story embodies a unique confluence: the intensive, specialized medical attention provided by a 'No. 1 Hospital in the World' alongside the robust 'No. 1 Student Experience' cultivated by her university. This dual engagement underscores the critical role of comprehensive support systems in enabling individuals to pursue higher education while managing profound personal health challenges. ### Background Anna Leigh Stang enrolled at Clemson University with aspirations to pursue a degree in philosophy. Like many students, her initial years involved adapting to university life and engaging with her chosen field of study. However, beneath the surface, Stang was grappling with a series of perplexing health issues that progressively impacted her daily life and academic performance. Eventually, she received a diagnosis of Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome (EDS), a genetic disorder affecting connective tissues, leading to symptoms such as joint hypermobility, skin fragility, and chronic pain. EDS often presents with co-occurring conditions, and in Stang's case, these included Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS), which affects blood circulation, and Mast Cell Activation Syndrome (MCAS), an immune disorder. The complexity and rarity of these conditions necessitated specialized medical expertise. ### Timeline **Early Years (Pre-University):** Anna Leigh Stang began experiencing subtle, undiagnosed health symptoms that progressively worsened over time. **Start of University (circa 2018-2019):** Stang enrolled at Clemson University, commencing her studies in philosophy. Her health challenges became more pronounced, affecting her ability to participate fully in campus life and sometimes requiring her to adjust her academic schedule. **Diagnosis Period (2019-2020):** Following extensive consultations and diagnostic tests, Stang received the formal diagnoses of Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome, Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome, and Mast Cell Activation Syndrome. This period marked a critical turning point, providing clarity regarding her complex medical situation. **Mayo Clinic Engagement (2020-Present):** Due to the specialized nature of her conditions, Stang sought treatment at the Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Minnesota. Her care involved multiple visits, extended stays for diagnostic evaluations, and ongoing treatment regimens. During this time, she strategically leveraged online learning options and received academic accommodations from Clemson University to continue her studies remotely or with adjusted workloads. **Continued Academic Progress (Present):** Despite ongoing medical management and the physical demands of her conditions, Stang remains an enrolled philosophy major at Clemson, making consistent progress toward her degree. Her academic journey has been uniquely shaped by the need to balance rigorous intellectual pursuits with intensive medical care. ### Latest Developments Anna Leigh Stang continues her academic work as a philosophy major at Clemson University, a testament to her determination and the support systems in place. Her health management remains an ongoing process, involving regular check-ups and adherence to treatment protocols established by the Mayo Clinic. While the challenges of her conditions persist, she has developed strategies for balancing her medical needs with her academic responsibilities. Clemson University has actively supported Stang through its disability services and academic advising, facilitating necessary accommodations, such as flexible deadlines, remote learning options, and access to assistive technologies. This ongoing collaboration between Stang, her medical team, and university staff exemplifies a tailored approach to student well-being and academic success in the face of significant adversity. ### Analysis Stang's experience highlights several critical aspects of contemporary higher education and healthcare.
