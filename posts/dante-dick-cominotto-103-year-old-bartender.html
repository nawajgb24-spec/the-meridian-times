Dante 'Dick' Cominotto, 103, Served Decades Behind the Bar, Leaving Enduring Legacy

Dante 'Dick' Cominotto, widely recognized as one of America's oldest active bartenders, passed away at the age of 103 in Kenosha, Wisconsin. Cominotto dedicated more than six decades to serving patrons at the same establishment, Stone's Throw tavern (formerly The Buzz Bar), where his quick wit, sharp memory, and unwavering presence made him a beloved community fixture. His extraordinary longevity in the hospitality industry, coupled with his service as a World War II veteran, garnered national attention, inspiring many with his commitment to work and life.

Kenosha, Wisconsin — Dante “Dick” Cominotto, a fixture behind the bar at Kenosha's Stone's Throw tavern for more than 60 years and widely considered one of the oldest active bartenders in the United States, passed away on November 25, 2023, at the remarkable age of 103. His passing marks the end of an era for the local community and the broader hospitality industry, which often celebrated his extraordinary dedication and vitality. Cominotto’s story transcended the typical bartender's tale, becoming a testament to enduring work ethic, community spirit, and the value of human connection across generations. He continued to serve patrons, mix drinks, and share stories at least once a week until just before his final days, embodying a commitment to his profession that inspired both regulars and international media outlets. ### Background Dante Cominotto was born on February 19, 1920, a year that saw the ratification of the 19th Amendment and the dawn of the Roaring Twenties. His life journey began in an era vastly different from the one he left, spanning over a century of profound societal and technological change. His early life, like that of many of his generation, was shaped by significant historical events. Cominotto honorably served his country in the U.S. Army during World War II, a period that instilled in him a strong sense of duty and resilience. Details of his service were often recounted in personal anecdotes, highlighting a life rich with experience even before his bartending career truly took hold. Following his military service, Cominotto returned to civilian life and eventually found his calling in the hospitality sector. He first began working at The Buzz Bar, a local establishment in Kenosha, in the 1960s. This was not merely a job for Cominotto; it became an integral part of his identity and a central pillar of his life for more than six decades. Even when The Buzz Bar changed ownership and was rebranded as Stone's Throw in 2012, Cominotto remained a constant presence. The new owners recognized the invaluable role he played in the bar's history and its community appeal, ensuring his continued employment. His ability to recall patrons’ drink preferences and share tales from decades past made him a living archive of Kenosha’s social history. ### Timeline **February 19, 1920:** Dante Cominotto is born in Kenosha, Wisconsin. **1940s:** Serves in the U.S. Army during World War II. **Circa 1960s:** Begins his career as a bartender at The Buzz Bar in Kenosha, Wisconsin, a role he would hold for over 60 years. **2012:** The Buzz Bar is purchased by new owners and rebranded as Stone's Throw. Cominotto continues to bartend under the new management. **February 2020:** Cominotto celebrates his 100th birthday, still actively working at Stone's Throw, drawing local and national media attention for his incredible longevity. **2023:** At 103 years old, he continues to work regular shifts, typically one or two evenings a week, maintaining his reputation as the nation’s oldest active bartender. **November 25, 2023:** Dante “Dick” Cominotto passes away peacefully at the age of 103, marking the end of his distinguished life and career. ### Latest Developments Cominotto remained actively employed at Stone's Throw, working part-time, until approximately a month before his passing. His final shift behind the bar was a testament to his enduring spirit and commitment. Despite his advanced age, he reportedly maintained his sharp mental faculties and engaging personality, continuing to charm customers with his stories and efficient service. His health began to decline in the weeks leading up to his death, leading to his eventual passing in late November. The news of his death prompted an outpouring of tributes from the Kenosha community and beyond, highlighting the significant impact he had on countless lives over his extensive career. ### Analysis Cominotto's career offers a unique case study in longevity and sustained engagement in the workforce. His ability to perform his duties effectively into his second century challenged conventional perceptions of retirement and active aging. His profession, requiring social interaction, memory, and physical presence, likely contributed to his mental and physical well-being. The hospitality industry, known for its demanding hours and physical requirements, rarely sees individuals maintain such a consistent presence for so long. Cominotto's story underscores the value of experience, the personal satisfaction derived from work, and the role of a supportive community and workplace in fostering a long and active life. His routine, which included walking to work, further speaks to his independence and resilience. ### Public Response The public response to Cominotto's life and career was overwhelmingly positive and admiring. His 100th birthday, and subsequent years of active bartending, attracted significant media attention from local, national, and even international news outlets. Features highlighted his wit, his wisdom, and his unique place in American culture, often showcasing him as a symbol of perseverance and joy in work. Upon his passing, social media platforms and local news comment sections were filled with tributes from former customers, community members, and admirers who had followed his story. Many shared personal anecdotes of their interactions with him, emphasizing his kind demeanor, his ability to remember names and stories, and the genuine connection he forged with those he served. He was remembered not just as a bartender, but as a friend, a mentor, and a living piece of Kenosha history. ### Industry Impact Cominotto's extended tenure had a notable, albeit informal, impact on the hospitality industry. His story served as an inspiration, prompting discussions about the value of experienced staff, the potential for flexible work arrangements for older workers, and the often-overlooked social role of local establishments. He demonstrated that age does not necessarily diminish one's capacity for valuable contribution, particularly in roles that thrive on interpersonal skills and institutional knowledge. His longevity also shone a light on the changing demographics of the workforce and the potential benefits of retaining experienced older employees. While not a formal policy shift, his example offered a compelling narrative for valuing diverse age groups in the service industry, emphasizing the unique perspective and stability that long-serving individuals can bring to a team and customer base. ### Future Outlook The physical presence of Dante Cominotto is irreplaceable at Stone's Throw, but his legacy is expected to endure. The bar now faces the challenge of continuing without its most iconic figure, yet it also inherits a rich narrative that will likely continue to attract visitors and sustain its unique charm. His story has become part of the tavern's identity, woven into its fabric and the memories of its patrons. Beyond Stone's Throw, Cominotto's narrative will likely persist as an example in broader discussions about active aging, the societal benefits of meaningful employment at all ages, and the importance of community hubs like local taverns. His life serves as a powerful reminder that purpose and connection can extend well beyond traditional retirement ages, offering a model for how individuals can continue to thrive and contribute. ### Conclusion Dante “Dick” Cominotto’s life was a testament to dedication, resilience, and the power of human connection. For over six decades, he was more than just a bartender; he was an anchor in the Kenosha community, a custodian of countless stories, and a source of comfort and conversation for generations of patrons. His extraordinary career, extending to his 103rd year, challenged perceptions of age and work, earning him a place in the hearts of many and in the annals of American hospitality. His quiet dedication and vibrant spirit left an indelible mark on Stone's Throw and the wider world. While the 'last call' has sounded for Dick Cominotto, the echoes of his life behind the bar, filled with anecdotes, expertly mixed drinks, and genuine warmth, will continue to resonate for years to come. He embodied a simple yet profound truth: that a life well-lived is often one spent in service, surrounded by people, and dedicated to a craft.
