China's Linglong One SMR Enters Crucial Hot Testing Phase

China's domestically developed Linglong One (ACP100) Small Modular Reactor (SMR) has commenced hot testing at the Changjiang Nuclear Power Plant in Hainan Province. This critical pre-operational phase involves running the reactor's primary systems at simulated operating temperatures and pressures without nuclear fuel, verifying their integrity and functionality. As the world's first commercial land-based SMR, the successful progression of Linglong One through this milestone marks a significant step towards its eventual commissioning, underscoring China's leadership in advanced nuclear technology and the global push for smaller, more versatile nuclear power solutions.

## China's Linglong One SMR Enters Crucial Hot Testing Phase **Introduction** China’s ambition in advanced nuclear technology has reached a significant milestone with the commencement of hot testing for the Linglong One Small Modular Reactor (SMR). Located at the Changjiang Nuclear Power Plant in Hainan Province, this domestically developed reactor, officially known as the ACP100, is undergoing a critical pre-operational phase that moves it closer to commissioning. Hot testing is a comprehensive functional performance test of the reactor’s primary circuit and associated systems under simulated operating conditions. This process involves circulating water at high temperatures and pressures, replicating the environment of an operational reactor but without the presence of nuclear fuel. Its successful completion is essential before fuel loading can proceed. **Background** The Linglong One project represents a significant global endeavor in the field of nuclear energy. Developed by the China National Nuclear Corporation (CNNC), the ACP100 design garnered international attention when it became the first SMR to pass a general safety review by the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) in 2016. This endorsement underscored the design's adherence to international safety standards. Small Modular Reactors are defined by their compact size, modular design, and potential for factory fabrication, offering advantages in terms of reduced construction time, lower capital costs, and enhanced safety features compared to traditional large-scale reactors. They are seen as a versatile solution for power generation, industrial heat, urban heating, and seawater desalination. **Timeline** The journey of the Linglong One SMR from concept to its current testing phase has been a multi-year undertaking. Following the IAEA safety review, the project gained momentum, reflecting China's strategic commitment to nuclear innovation. Construction of the Linglong One demonstration project officially commenced in July 2021, marking the physical realization of the SMR. In October 2022, a major construction milestone was achieved with the successful installation of the reactor's containment dome, signaling progress towards the completion of the physical structure. Prior to hot testing, the reactor underwent cold functional tests. These tests, typically completed in late 2023 or early 2024, involved circulating coolant at ambient temperatures and pressures to verify piping integrity and component functionality. The initiation of hot testing marks the transition to a more advanced stage of pre-operational verification. **Latest Developments** The focus of recent news has centered on the initiation of hot functional testing for the Linglong One. This phase involves gradually increasing the temperature and pressure within the reactor's primary coolant system to match operational conditions, utilizing heat generated by running main coolant pumps and other auxiliary systems. During this period, engineers and technicians meticulously monitor hundreds of parameters. These include pressure and temperature readings, flow rates, vibration levels, and the performance of control and safety systems. The objective is to confirm that all components, from the reactor pressure vessel to the intricate network of pipes and valves, function as designed under the thermal and hydraulic stresses of operation. Successful completion of hot testing will validate the integrated performance of the entire nuclear island, proving its readiness to safely contain and manage the heat generated during actual nuclear fission. This validation is a mandatory step before the loading of nuclear fuel, which will then pave the way for achieving criticality and eventually connecting to the grid. **Analysis** The progression of the Linglong One to hot testing highlights China's methodical approach to nuclear development and its ambition to be a frontrunner in advanced reactor technologies. The SMR concept offers potential advantages in terms of flexibility and distributed power generation, crucial for regions with smaller grid capacities or specific energy demands. Furthermore, the modular nature of SMRs could revolutionize construction processes, potentially reducing overall project timelines and costs compared to traditional large reactors. This scalability makes them attractive for a diverse range of applications beyond electricity generation, including providing process heat for heavy industries and contributing to water desalination efforts. The project’s success could serve as a blueprint for future SMR deployments both within China and globally. It demonstrates a practical application of the SMR technology, moving it beyond theoretical discussions and into tangible operational phases. This provides valuable data and experience for the entire nuclear industry. **Public Response** Public discourse surrounding nuclear energy, including SMRs, often centers on safety, environmental impact, and economic viability. While specific public responses regarding the hot testing phase of Linglong One in China are not widely reported in international media, general public sentiment towards nuclear power in China has been largely supportive, particularly in the context of energy security and carbon reduction goals. Globally, SMR technology is often presented as having enhanced safety features, including passive safety systems that do not rely on active intervention or external power for shutdown in an emergency. Proponents argue that the smaller size and simpler design of SMRs contribute to their inherent safety. However, concerns about waste management and potential proliferation risks remain common themes in broader public discussions about nuclear energy. **Industry Impact** The successful hot testing of Linglong One is expected to have a substantial impact on the global nuclear industry. It provides tangible proof of concept for a commercial land-based SMR, potentially accelerating development and deployment efforts by other nations and companies. China's progress could spur increased investment and research into SMR technology, fostering greater competition and innovation in the sector. It may also influence regulatory frameworks globally, as authorities observe the licensing and operational experiences of Linglong One. The operational data gathered will be invaluable for refining designs and validating performance claims for future SMR projects. This development strengthens China's position as a key player in the international nuclear energy landscape, demonstrating its capacity not only for building large-scale reactors but also for innovating in the advanced reactor space. It could open doors for Chinese nuclear technology exports in the SMR market. **Future Outlook** With hot testing underway, the next critical steps for Linglong One include fuel loading, achieving initial criticality, and eventually connecting to the electricity grid. These phases are projected to occur in the coming years, with full commercial operation anticipated potentially by 2025. The long-term outlook for Linglong One and SMR technology in general appears promising. As nations worldwide grapple with the dual challenges of increasing energy demand and climate change, SMRs offer a potential solution for clean, reliable, and flexible power. Further deployment of SMRs in various applications, including remote communities and industrial complexes, is expected to become more widespread. The experience gained from Linglong One will be crucial in informing the design and deployment of future SMRs, not just in China but globally. It will help establish best practices for construction, operation, and maintenance, shaping the future trajectory of nuclear energy. **Conclusion** The initiation of hot testing for China's Linglong One SMR represents a pivotal moment in the global development of advanced nuclear technology. As the world's first commercial land-based SMR, its successful progression through this rigorous verification phase underscores significant engineering and scientific achievements. This milestone not only propels China's nuclear energy program forward but also provides a vital real-world case study for the burgeoning SMR industry, offering a glimpse into a future where nuclear power is more modular, versatile, and accessible.
