## Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center Earns National Recognition from U.S. News & World Report **BOSTON, MA –** Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (BIDMC) has once again been nationally recognized by U.S. News & World Report, an acknowledgment that underscores its ongoing commitment to high-quality patient care, advanced medical research, and professional excellence. The annual U.S. News & World Report 'Best Hospitals' rankings serve as a widely referenced guide for patients seeking exceptional medical care, particularly for challenging conditions or complex surgeries. For BIDMC, an academic medical center affiliated with Harvard Medical School, this recognition is a testament to its dedicated staff, cutting-edge facilities, and patient-centric approach. ### Background on Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center holds a significant place in the Boston healthcare ecosystem and beyond. Formed in 1996 through the merger of Beth Israel Hospital and New England Deaconess Hospital, BIDMC quickly established itself as a leading academic medical center. Its rich history dates back to the early 20th century, built on foundations of compassionate care and medical innovation. As a major teaching hospital of Harvard Medical School, BIDMC is at the forefront of medical education, training the next generation of healthcare professionals. It is also a global leader in biomedical research, with investigators making significant contributions across a wide spectrum of diseases and health conditions. This integrated approach to patient care, research, and education is fundamental to its sustained high performance. BIDMC's mission extends beyond direct patient treatment, encompassing a broader commitment to advancing medical knowledge and improving public health. Its robust clinical programs cover nearly all adult specialties, supported by a strong emphasis on patient safety and the adoption of advanced medical technologies. ### Timeline of Recognition The U.S. News & World Report 'Best Hospitals' rankings were first introduced in 1989, quickly becoming an authoritative source for evaluating hospital performance.
