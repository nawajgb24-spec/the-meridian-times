U.S. to Co-Host 2031 Women's World Cup; UK Awarded 2035 Tournament

FIFA has announced that the United States will co-host the 2031 Women's World Cup, marking its return as a host nation, while the United Kingdom has been selected for the 2035 edition. These landmark decisions were made on December 10, 2024, following a comprehensive bidding process. The selections underscore FIFA's commitment to advancing women's football globally, leveraging the robust infrastructure and growing fan bases in both regions to elevate the sport to new heights. The tournaments are anticipated to deliver significant economic and social benefits, further solidifying the Women's World Cup as a premier global sporting event.

### Introduction FIFA, the international governing body of football, announced on December 10, 2024, its selections for the host nations of the 2031 and 2035 Women's World Cups. The United States has been awarded the right to co-host the 2031 tournament, signifying a strategic return to a nation with a deep history in women's football. Four years later, the United Kingdom will make its debut as a host for the 2035 edition, a significant milestone for the sport in the region. These decisions follow an extensive bidding process, with FIFA prioritizing nations capable of delivering world-class events while also maximizing the growth and commercial potential of women's football. The selections reflect the ongoing global expansion and increasing prominence of the Women's World Cup. ### Background Since its inception in 1991, the FIFA Women's World Cup has grown exponentially, evolving from a nascent tournament into a global spectacle. The United States has played a pivotal role in this growth, famously hosting the event in 1999. That tournament set attendance records and captivated a nation, leading to a surge in women's football participation and fan interest. The U.S. also stepped in to host the 2003 edition due to a public health crisis in China. In contrast, the United Kingdom, despite its rich footballing heritage, has never hosted a FIFA Women's World Cup. However, the women's game in the UK has experienced a significant renaissance in recent years, particularly with the professionalization and growing popularity of the Women's Super League (WSL). This burgeoning domestic scene and strong public interest provided a compelling case for its 2035 bid. ### Timeline The journey to these host selections involved several stages. FIFA initiated the bidding process for both the 2031 and 2035 tournaments in late 2023, inviting member associations to express their interest. Following the successful award of the 2027 Women's World Cup to Brazil in May 2024, attention shifted to the subsequent editions. Throughout the third quarter of 2024, formal bid submissions were received from various interested nations. FIFA's technical committees then undertook a comprehensive evaluation period in the fourth quarter, assessing each bid based on infrastructure, commercial strategy, operational capabilities, and commitment to human rights and sustainability. These evaluations culminated in the final recommendations presented to the FIFA Council, leading to the official announcement on December 10, 2024. ### Latest Developments The announcement from FIFA Headquarters in Zurich confirms years of preparation and strategic planning by the U.S. Soccer Federation (USSF) and The Football Association (FA) in the UK. FIFA President Gianni Infantino commended both host nations for their ambitious bids, highlighting their potential to deliver exceptional tournaments. Cindy Parlow Cone, President of the U.S. Soccer Federation, expressed enthusiasm for bringing the tournament back to American soil, emphasizing the commitment to showcasing the best of women's football. Mark Bullingham, Chief Executive of The FA, spoke of the immense pride and opportunity for the United Kingdom to host such a prestigious global event for the first time. ### Analysis FIFA's decision to award the 2031 tournament to the United States reflects a strategic choice to leverage an established market with proven capabilities for large-scale sporting events. The U.S. possesses unparalleled infrastructure, including numerous modern stadia, extensive transportation networks, and a vast commercial landscape. The strong existing fan base and successful domestic league (NWSL) promise significant attendance figures and broadcast revenue, aligning with FIFA's objectives for maximizing commercial returns and global viewership. The selection of the United Kingdom for 2035 represents a different, yet equally strategic, pathway. While the UK has not previously hosted the Women's World Cup, its football infrastructure is world-class, and the growth of women's football in the country has been remarkable. The bid likely emphasized the potential for rapid expansion of the sport, creating a lasting legacy and tapping into a passionate, albeit relatively newer, segment of the global women's football audience. This decision signals FIFA's dual approach: solidifying the base in strong markets and fostering growth in developing ones. ### Public Response The news has been met with widespread excitement across both the United States and the United Kingdom. In the U.S., fans, players, and officials expressed anticipation for the return of the tournament that holds a special place in the nation's sporting history. Social media platforms quickly buzzed with reactions, celebrating the opportunity to witness elite women's football on home ground once again. In the UK, the announcement was met with elation. Football associations, grassroots clubs, and supporters hailed it as a transformative moment for women's football in the country. Advocates believe the 2035 tournament will inspire a new generation of players and further embed the sport into the national consciousness, building upon the success of the Lionesses in recent international competitions. Local governments and tourism bodies have also voiced strong support, recognizing the potential for significant economic benefits. ### Industry Impact The economic implications for both host nations are expected to be substantial. Hosting a FIFA Women's World Cup involves massive investments in infrastructure, hospitality, and security, creating numerous jobs and boosting local economies. The U.S., with its experience in hosting mega-events, is poised to attract record numbers of international visitors, driving tourism revenue and spending across various sectors. For the UK, the 2035 tournament offers a unique opportunity to accelerate investment in women's sports facilities and programs. Sponsorship deals, media rights, and merchandise sales are anticipated to reach new highs, further professionalizing the sport. The increased visibility will also likely translate into greater participation at all levels, from grassroots to elite academies, and attract more investment into domestic leagues like the NWSL and WSL, enhancing their global standing. ### Future Outlook With over six years until 2031 and a decade until 2035, the preparatory phases for both tournaments will now commence in earnest. Host committees in the U.S. and UK will work closely with FIFA to develop detailed operational plans, identify host cities, and initiate infrastructure upgrades where necessary. This will involve collaboration with various stakeholders, including national and local governments, sports organizations, and community groups. Key areas of focus will include sustainability initiatives, fan engagement strategies, and legacy planning to ensure that the tournaments leave a lasting positive impact beyond the final whistle. The aim is to build upon the success of previous Women's World Cups, setting new benchmarks for attendance, viewership, and overall experience, and further cementing women's football as a global powerhouse. ### Conclusion The awarding of the 2031 FIFA Women's World Cup to the United States and the 2035 edition to the United Kingdom represents a pivotal moment in the ongoing evolution of women's football. These selections reflect FIFA's strategic vision to expand the sport's global footprint, capitalizing on the robust infrastructure and enthusiastic fan bases in proven markets while simultaneously fostering growth in nations poised for significant development. As the countdown to these landmark events begins, the global football community anticipates two exceptional tournaments that will undoubtedly inspire millions and further elevate the status of women's sport on the international stage.
