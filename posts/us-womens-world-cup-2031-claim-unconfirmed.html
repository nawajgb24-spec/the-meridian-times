US Hosting 2031 Women's World Cup: Trump Official's Claim Unconfirmed by FIFA

A claim by former U.S. National Security Advisor Robert O'Brien that the United States will host the 2031 FIFA Women's World Cup has emerged, coinciding with FIFA's announcement of Brazil as the 2027 host. While O'Brien stated confidently that the U.S. would secure the tournament, FIFA has not officially opened the bidding process for 2031, nor has it confirmed any host. U.S. Soccer previously withdrew its 2027 bid to focus on 2031, indicating a strong national interest, but any official award remains subject to FIFA's formal procedures.

### Introduction Former U.S. National Security Advisor Robert O'Brien has stated that the United States is set to host the FIFA Women's World Cup in 2031. The declaration, made to Reuters on May 17, 2024, has introduced a new dynamic into the future planning of women's international football. O'Brien indicated that discussions with FIFA President Gianni Infantino had taken place, expressing confidence in the U.S. securing the major tournament. His remarks arrived on the same day that FIFA officially announced Brazil as the host nation for the 2027 Women's World Cup during its Congress in Bangkok. Despite O'Brien's assertion, FIFA has not publicly opened the bidding process for the 2031 event, nor has it made any official confirmation regarding a host for that year. ### Background The United States has a significant history with the FIFA Women's World Cup, having hosted the tournament twice before. The 1999 edition, a landmark event for women's sports, culminated in the U.S. national team's victory in a memorable final at the Rose Bowl, drawing record attendance and widespread national attention. The U.S. also stepped in as an emergency host in 2003 when the tournament was relocated from China due to the SARS outbreak. These experiences underscore the nation's capacity and infrastructure for managing large-scale international sporting events. Prior to O'Brien's statement, U.S. Soccer, in conjunction with the Mexican Football Federation, had initially pursued a joint bid to host the 2027 Women's World Cup. However, in April 2024, both federations formally withdrew their bid. At the time, U.S. Soccer articulated a strategic pivot, stating their intention to concentrate efforts on a bid for the 2031 tournament instead. ### Timeline **1999:** The United States successfully hosts the FIFA Women's World Cup, marking a significant moment for the sport globally. **2003:** The U.S. steps in to host the Women's World Cup after the event is moved from China. **April 2024:** U.S. Soccer and the Mexican Football Federation jointly withdraw their bid for the 2027 Women's World Cup. They announce their intention to focus on securing the 2031 tournament. **May 17, 2024:** During its Congress in Bangkok, FIFA officially awards the hosting rights for the 2027 Women's World Cup to Brazil, marking the first time the tournament will be held in South America. **May 17, 2024:** Robert O'Brien, a former U.S. National Security Advisor, informs Reuters that the United States is slated to host the 2031 Women's World Cup, citing discussions with FIFA President Gianni Infantino. ### Latest Developments The most recent development centers on Robert O'Brien's interview with Reuters, where he unequivocally stated, "The U.S. is going to host the Women's World Cup in 2031." He elaborated that this outcome was discussed with FIFA President Gianni Infantino and that "we're going to get it." The timing of these comments is particularly noteworthy, as they occurred on the same day FIFA selected Brazil to host the 2027 event. Despite O'Brien's strong assertion, FIFA has not issued any official statement confirming the United States as the 2031 host. The global governing body for football typically conducts a formal bidding process, which includes a request for proposals, evaluation, and a final vote by the FIFA Council or Congress. For 2031, this process has not yet been initiated publicly, indicating that O'Brien's statement is, at this juncture, an informal claim rather than an official award. U.S. Soccer has not commented directly on O'Brien's specific statement, but its earlier public stance to pursue the 2031 bid remains consistent with the aspiration to host the tournament. ### Analysis O'Brien's statement, while not an official FIFA declaration, highlights a strong desire within certain U.S. circles to bring the Women's World Cup back to American soil. His past role as National Security Advisor suggests a level of access to high-level international discussions, yet the absence of a parallel announcement from FIFA is significant. Such a pronouncement could serve multiple purposes. It might be an attempt to apply early pressure, a public negotiation tactic, or an expression of confidence based on preliminary talks that are not yet formalized. The United States offers unparalleled infrastructure, stadium capacity, and a proven track record for successfully staging major sporting events, including the upcoming 2026 Men's World Cup (co-hosted with Mexico and Canada). Economically, a U.S.-hosted Women's World Cup is projected to generate substantial revenue through ticket sales, tourism, merchandise, and sponsorships. The burgeoning popularity of women's soccer in the U.S., evidenced by growing viewership and league attendance, further bolsters the nation's appeal as a host. ### Public Response The public response to O'Brien's statement has been somewhat muted, primarily due to the unofficial nature of the claim. However, the prospect of the U.S. hosting another Women's World Cup is likely to be met with considerable enthusiasm among sports fans and the broader public, given the success and cultural impact of past tournaments, particularly the 1999 event. For followers of women's soccer, the idea of the sport's premier global competition returning to the U.S. represents an opportunity for further growth and engagement. Supporters would likely anticipate a high-quality event with strong attendance and media coverage, building on the momentum generated by recent tournaments and the success of the U.S. Women's National Team. However, the lack of official confirmation from FIFA means that any immediate public celebration or widespread reaction is tempered by the understanding that the process is still in its nascent stages. ### Industry Impact Should the United States ultimately secure the hosting rights for the 2031 Women's World Cup, the impact across various industries would be substantial. The hospitality and tourism sectors would experience a significant boost, with an influx of international visitors contributing to local economies through hotel stays, dining, and other leisure activities. Infrastructure, including transportation networks and stadium facilities, would also see investment and utilization. The sports marketing and media industries would find lucrative opportunities in broadcasting rights, sponsorship deals, and merchandising. The tournament would provide a massive platform for brands to align with women's sports, tapping into a growing and increasingly influential demographic of fans and consumers. Furthermore, the event could inspire further grassroots participation and professional development within women's soccer nationwide, fostering long-term growth for the sport. ### Future Outlook The future outlook for the 2031 FIFA Women's World Cup hosting rights remains contingent on FIFA's official procedures. While O'Brien's statement signals strong U.S. interest and confidence, the next critical step will be FIFA's formal opening of the bidding process. This typically involves an invitation for member associations to express interest, followed by detailed bid submissions, evaluations, and eventual selection by FIFA's decision-making bodies. The U.S., potentially with Mexico as a co-host, is expected to be a formidable contender, given its infrastructure, economic power, and successful hosting history. However, other nations or regional blocs are also likely to emerge as bidders, ensuring a competitive process. The ultimate decision will likely be made within the next two to three years, setting the stage for preparations for the 2031 tournament. ### Conclusion Robert O'Brien's assertion that the United States will host the 2031 FIFA Women's World Cup has put the prospect firmly on the international sports agenda. While the statement reflects a clear national ambition and confidence stemming from high-level discussions, it remains unconfirmed by FIFA's official channels. The global football body is yet to open the formal bidding process for 2031, having just awarded the 2027 tournament to Brazil. The U.S. has demonstrated its capability to host the Women's World Cup successfully on two prior occasions, and U.S. Soccer has publicly shifted its focus from 2027 to a 2031 bid. The journey towards securing the 2031 Women's World Cup will require navigating FIFA's established protocols and likely competing against other interested nations. For now, the prospect remains a highly anticipated, yet officially unconfirmed, possibility for the U.S.
