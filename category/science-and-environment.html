A Global Journey Through Earth's Icy Epochs

Earth's history is profoundly shaped by periodic ice ages, vast periods when glaciers advanced across continents. This article explores these 'ages of ice,' tracing their causes, global impacts on geology, climate, and life, and their enduring legacy. From ancient 'Snowball Earth' events to the Quaternary Glaciation that includes our current era, understanding these icy epochs offers a unique perspective on Earth's dynamic past and its future climate trajectory.

Parachute Science Undermines Tropical Insect Conservation Efforts

The pervasive practice of 'parachute science' is increasingly identified as a critical impediment to understanding and conserving tropical insect populations. Researchers from high-income nations often conduct studies in biodiverse regions, collecting data and samples with limited meaningful collaboration or benefit to local scientists and communities. This approach stifles local scientific development, skews research priorities, and creates significant gaps in long-term ecological data crucial for addressing insect declines. Calls for more equitable, collaborative, and decolonized scientific partnerships are growing, aiming to empower local expertise and foster sustainable conservation strategies.

Global Initiative Buries Underwear for Science: Assessing Soil Health

Around the world, a unique citizen science project known as 'Soil Your Undies' is utilizing 100% cotton underwear to provide a visual and accessible assessment of soil health. By burying the garments for a period of months, participants can observe the degree of decomposition, which serves as a direct indicator of microbial activity and the vitality of local soil ecosystems. This global effort aims to engage the public and agricultural communities in understanding, monitoring, and improving soil health, highlighting its critical role in sustainable agriculture, environmental stability, and climate resilience.

JCB Hydromax Shatters Hydrogen Land Speed Record at 103.6 mph

JCB, the British construction equipment giant, has successfully set a new world land speed record for a hydrogen-powered vehicle. The specially modified JCB Hydromax, driven by Guy Martin, reached 103.6 mph at Elvington Airfield, demonstrating the potential of hydrogen internal combustion engine technology for rapid decarbonization. This achievement underscores JCB's substantial investment in hydrogen power as a viable, zero-emission alternative, particularly for heavy machinery.
