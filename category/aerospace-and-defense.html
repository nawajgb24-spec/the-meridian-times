Army-Backed Zephyr Drone Shatters World Flight Endurance Record

The Airbus Zephyr S, a high-altitude, solar-powered drone supported by the U.S. Army, has established a new world record for continuous uncrewed flight. Remaining airborne for over 64 days in the stratosphere during summer 2022, the drone demonstrated unprecedented endurance, pushing the boundaries of persistent aerial surveillance and communication capabilities. This achievement marks a pivotal moment in the development of 'pseudo-satellite' technology, offering a strategic advantage for both military and potential civil applications by providing prolonged, localized coverage without the complexities of space-based assets.

Tobyhanna Assembled Engines Power Drone to World-Record Flight

Tobyhanna Army Depot played a pivotal role in a groundbreaking achievement in aviation, contributing to a drone's world-record endurance flight. Technicians at the Pennsylvania depot assembled and modified the heavy-fuel engines for the Vanilla Aircraft VA001, which subsequently achieved an unprecedented 121-hour, 24-minute unrefueled flight in early 2017. This milestone underscored the depot's specialized capabilities and significantly advanced the potential for long-duration unmanned intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) missions.
