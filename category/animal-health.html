Decades of Vigilance: The Fight Against the New World Screwworm

The United States Department of Agriculture's Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) leads a long-standing, globally recognized effort to prevent the re-establishment of the New World Screwworm (Cochliomyia hominivorax). This parasitic fly, once a scourge of livestock and wildlife across the Americas, was eradicated from North and Central America through the innovative Sterile Insect Technique (SIT). Despite its success, constant surveillance, robust diagnostic capabilities, and the critical Panama barrier zone remain essential to protect animal health and prevent devastating economic and ecological impacts. APHIS's swift response to the 2016 Florida Keys outbreak underscored the ongoing threat and the necessity of sustained vigilance.

New World Screwworm: Vigilance Continues Decades After Eradication

The New World Screwworm, a notorious livestock pest once eradicated from North and Central America, remains a focus of intense surveillance and prevention efforts. Decades after the successful implementation of the Sterile Insect Technique (SIT), federal agencies and international partners maintain a permanent barrier in Panama's Darien Gap to prevent reintroduction from endemic South American regions. The 2016 Florida Keys outbreak served as a stark reminder of the persistent threat and the necessity of rapid, coordinated responses to protect agricultural economies, wildlife, and public health.

Second Screwworm Case Confirmed in Grant County, New Mexico

Authorities have confirmed a second case of New World screwworm in Grant County, New Mexico, amplifying concerns for both livestock and wildlife. This parasitic pest, once eradicated from the U.S., was first detected in the state in October 2023. Federal and state agencies are intensifying surveillance and control measures to contain the spread and prevent re-establishment of the highly damaging insect.
