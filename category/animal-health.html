New World Screwworm: Vigilance Continues Decades After Eradication

The New World Screwworm, a notorious livestock pest once eradicated from North and Central America, remains a focus of intense surveillance and prevention efforts. Decades after the successful implementation of the Sterile Insect Technique (SIT), federal agencies and international partners maintain a permanent barrier in Panama's Darien Gap to prevent reintroduction from endemic South American regions. The 2016 Florida Keys outbreak served as a stark reminder of the persistent threat and the necessity of rapid, coordinated responses to protect agricultural economies, wildlife, and public health.

Texas Confirms First Equine Screwworm Case Since 1966

Texas has reported its first equine infection of the New World screwworm in decades, identified in a ranch horse in Val Verde County. This confirmation by federal and state authorities has triggered immediate biosecurity protocols, including movement restrictions and enhanced surveillance, to contain the highly destructive parasite. The case marks a significant concern for the state's vast livestock industry, rekindling memories of past eradication efforts and underscoring the ongoing threat posed by the screwworm, which remains endemic in neighboring regions.

Texas Lifts Screwworm Quarantines in Border Counties

Quarantine restrictions in Cameron and Hidalgo Counties, Texas, imposed due to New World screwworm detections, have been lifted by the USDA. This announcement, made by Congressman Vicente Gonzalez, signifies a successful eradication effort for the parasitic pest that threatened livestock and wildlife. The multi-agency response utilized the Sterile Insect Technique to contain and eliminate the screwworm, which had been absent from the continental U.S. for decades, bringing relief to the agricultural sector.
