U.S. State Department Art: Scrutiny Over Collections and Diplomacy

Recent discussions have brought renewed attention to the U.S. State Department's Art in Embassies program, prompting questions about the selection, display, and occasional removal of artwork in diplomatic missions. This article explores the program's role in cultural diplomacy, examines the historical context of art controversies in government spaces, and analyzes the implications for artistic freedom and international relations. It delves into the various perspectives surrounding the management of these significant cultural assets and considers the potential for future shifts in policy or practice.

Global Conflicts Imperil Six Priceless World Heritage Sites

Ongoing conflicts and crises worldwide are placing an increasing number of UNESCO World Heritage sites at critical risk. This report highlights the plight of six specific sites—the Old City of Sana'a, Ancient City of Aleppo, Historic Centre of Odesa, Timbuktu, Bamiyan Valley, and Leptis Magna—each facing unique threats ranging from deliberate destruction and collateral damage to looting and neglect. International organizations are racing against time to protect these irreplaceable cultural treasures amidst active hostilities and instability.
