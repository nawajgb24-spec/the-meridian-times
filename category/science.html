Ancient 'Lost World' Challenges First American Migration Theories

New scientific understanding of a now-submerged ancient landscape along the Pacific Rim is reshaping theories on how the first humans arrived in the Americas around 13,000 years ago. This 'lost world,' encompassing the vast Beringian land bridge and coastal corridors, increasingly suggests that early populations may have followed a maritime route, utilizing rich marine resources, potentially challenging the long-held 'Clovis First' land-based migration model. Interdisciplinary research, combining archaeology, genetics, and oceanography, is now focused on uncovering the secrets hidden beneath the waves, potentially rewriting a foundational chapter of human history.
