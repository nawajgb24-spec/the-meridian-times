AP Embraces Vertical Vision: Reshaping Photojournalism for the Mobile Age

The Associated Press, a cornerstone of global news reporting, has strategically adapted its visual storytelling for the digital era by increasingly featuring vertical photography. This shift, exemplified by collections like 'See the world in vertical,' acknowledges the dominance of smartphone viewing and the unique narrative power of portrait-oriented images. AP photojournalists are at the forefront of this evolution, demonstrating how compelling stories can be captured and conveyed effectively within the vertical frame, influencing how news is consumed and visual information is processed worldwide.

Colorado Photographer Stan Honda's Iconic 9/11 Images Defined Ground Zero

On September 11, 2001, photojournalist Stan Honda, then with Agence France-Presse, captured some of the earliest and most enduring images from Ground Zero. His raw, immediate photographs provided the world its first stark glimpse of the World Trade Center's destruction, shaping global understanding of the attacks. Though based in New York, Honda later brought his journalistic eye to Colorado, where he continued his distinguished career, leaving a profound legacy in photojournalism and historical documentation.
